## Slides

### Slide 1
**Headline:** Why do you see spots after looking at a bright light?
**Setup:** The flash is over in an instant. The spot it leaves is not.
**Text:** Someone takes your photo with a flash, and for the next few seconds a dark spot floats in the middle of everything you look at. Look at the wall: it's there. Look at the floor: it's there too. But there's nothing on the wall or the floor.
**Hook:** So where is the spot?
**Picture:** A face lit by a flash. Then a dark spot hangs over a wall; the view swings down to a floor and the spot is still there. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** FLASH · DARK SPOT · WALL · FLOOR

### Slide 2
**Headline:** A flash tires out a patch of cells in your eye
**Text:** It's in your eye. At the back of each eye is a layer of cells that turn light into signals for your brain. The flash hits a patch of those cells so hard that they get tired out. For a little while, they respond less to light than the cells around them.
**Hook:** So why does a tired patch show up as a dark spot?
**Picture:** A side view of an eye. Light from a flash lands on the layer of cells at the back; one patch dims while the cells around it stay bright. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** FLASH · LAYER OF CELLS AT THE BACK OF YOUR EYE · TIRED CELLS

### Slide 3
**Headline:** Tired cells send weaker signals, so you see a dark spot
**Text:** Look at a plain wall. The cells around the patch send signals for the wall as usual, but the tired cells send weaker ones. Your brain gets a normal picture with a dark spot in it.
**Hook:** So why does the spot follow your eyes?
**Picture:** The same layer of cells as a grid facing a plain wall. Cells around the patch send strong signals up to a picture; the tired cells send faint ones. The picture fills in with a dark spot exactly where the tired cells are. This is the weak step: the spot in the picture must be built from those same cells. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** NORMAL CELLS: STRONG SIGNAL · TIRED CELLS: WEAK SIGNAL · WHAT YOU SEE

### Slide 4
**Headline:** The spot follows your eyes because it's part of your eye
**Text:** It's tied to that part of your eye, not to anything out there. A few seconds later, when the cells recover, it fades.
**Bold:** The spot isn't in the room. It's a patch of your eye that got tired out by the flash.
**Picture:** An eye looking at a scene. The tired patch stays on the same cells as the eye turns, so the dark spot slides across the scene with it. Then the patch brightens and the spot fades. The topic's one hands-on control: drag to turn the eye.
**Picture labels:** TIRED CELLS · DRAG TO LOOK AROUND · CELLS RECOVER: SPOT FADES

### Go deeper
Look at the spot against a far wall and it seems big; against your hand it seems small. The patch on your eye never changes size. Only your brain's guess at its size does.
