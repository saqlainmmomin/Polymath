## Slides

### Slide 1
**Headline:** Why is the sky blue at noon and red at sunset?
**Setup:** It's the same sky and the same Sun.
**Text:** The Sun looks yellow, but its light is really all colours mixed together. Air looks clear, but it isn't quite.
**Hook:** So how does clear air turn sunlight into a blue sky, and later a red and orange sunset?
**Picture:** The Sun above a patch of clear air. Its light is a band of mixed colours. Below the air, a blue sky on one side and a red and orange sunset on the other, each with a question mark. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** THE SUN · ALL COLOURS MIXED · CLEAR AIR · NOON · SUNSET

### Slide 2
**Headline:** Air is made of molecules that scatter light in all directions
**Text:** Light travels in waves, and each colour has its own length. Blue waves are short and red waves are long. Air is made of molecules, tiny bits of gas far too small to see. They scatter light, which means they send it off in all directions.
**Hook:** So does air scatter blue light the same as red light?
**Picture:** A short blue wave and a long red wave arrive at one molecule of air. The molecule sends light off in every direction, drawn as arrows fanning out. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** BLUE WAVE: SHORT · RED WAVE: LONG · MOLECULE OF AIR · SCATTERED IN ALL DIRECTIONS

### Slide 3
**Headline:** Air scatters blue about four times as strongly as red, so the blue comes from all over the sky
**Text:** Air scatters blue about four times as strongly as red, so far more blue than red is sent off. That scattered blue reaches your eyes from all over the sky. The sky has no blue of its own. The blue was in the sunlight all along.
**Hook:** So why is a sunset red and orange?
**Picture:** Sunlight crosses the air. Many blue arrows fan out from the air in every direction, and some reach an eye on the ground from different parts of the sky. A few red arrows are scattered too, but far fewer. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** SUNLIGHT · AIR · BLUE: ABOUT FOUR TIMES AS STRONGLY AS RED · RED · YOUR EYES · ALL OVER THE SKY

### Slide 4
**Headline:** At sunset the Sun's light slants through far more air, so red and orange are what is left
**Text:** At sunset the Sun is low, so its light slants through far more air. Right at the horizon, that is about 38 times as much air as when the Sun is straight overhead. On the way, much of the blue is scattered off in other directions and never reaches you. Red and orange are what is left. The Sun looks yellow at noon for the same reason. The air has already scattered some blue out of its light.
**Bold:** The same scattering that makes the sky blue takes much of the blue out of the sunset.
**Picture:** A Sun straight overhead sends a short straight path down through the air to an eye. A low Sun sends a long slanted path through far more air. Much of the blue peels off sideways along the long path, so red and orange are what is left at the eye. Only the Sun and the sky near it go orange and red. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** SUN OVERHEAD · LOW SUN · FAR MORE AIR · BLUE SCATTERED OFF · RED AND ORANGE · YOUR EYES
