## Slides

### Slide 1
**Headline:** Why does your voice sound different on a recording?
**Setup:** You know how you sound. Then you play it back.
**Text:** Play a recording of yourself and it feels wrong: that doesn't sound like me. But that's the way your voice reaches everyone else, and always has.
**Hook:** So which is the odd one out, the recording or the voice you hear when you talk?
**Picture:** A person talks. A speech bubble labelled "the voice you hear when you talk" sits beside a second bubble from a phone's playback labelled "the recording", and the person frowns at the second. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** THE VOICE YOU HEAR WHEN YOU TALK · THE RECORDING

### Slide 2
**Headline:** When you talk, your voice reaches your ears two ways
**Text:** Some goes out through the air and into your ears, like any sound. The rest travels as vibrations inside your head, through bone and flesh. Your friends only get the air route.
**Hook:** So what does the inside route do to your voice?
**Picture:** A head seen from the side. Sound leaves the mouth and curves round through the air to the ear (air route); a second line runs inside the head from mouth to ear (inside route). A friend stands opposite with only the air route reaching them. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** AIR ROUTE · INSIDE ROUTE: BONE AND FLESH · YOUR FRIEND GETS ONLY THE AIR ROUTE

### Slide 3
**Headline:** The inside route makes your own voice fuller
**Text:** It carries the low, deep part of a voice well. Put your fingers in your ears and talk: your voice turns boomy, because blocking the air route leaves the inside one louder.
**Hook:** So what does a recording pick up?
**Picture:** A voice drawn as two bands, a low deep one and a high one. The air route carries both to the ear; the inside route passes the low band mostly and the ear hears a fuller sound. Then two fingers plug the ear: the air route closes and the inside route's low band gets louder. The topic's one hands-on control: drag fingers to the ear. This is the weak step: the low-and-high split must be obvious.
**Picture labels:** LOW, DEEP PART · HIGH PART · INSIDE ROUTE · FINGERS IN EARS: BOOMY

### Slide 4
**Headline:** A microphone only picks up the air route
**Text:** A microphone sits outside your head, so it only picks up the air route, the same one your friends get. So the recording isn't the odd one out. Your live voice is, because only you get the extra route through your head.
**Bold:** Everyone hears your voice through the air. Only you hear it through your head too.
**Picture:** A microphone beside the speaker's head with an arrow for the air route only; a friend beside it, same arrow. The speaker's own ear shows both routes, with the extra inside route glowing. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** MICROPHONE: AIR ROUTE ONLY · YOUR FRIEND: AIR ROUTE ONLY · YOU: AIR ROUTE AND INSIDE ROUTE

### Go deeper
Bone-conduction headphones play sound through the bones of your skull to your inner ear, which leaves your ears free to hear the room. It's the same inside route your own voice uses, now used on purpose.
