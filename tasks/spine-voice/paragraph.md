# Voice on a recording, stage 2 (paragraph for owner approval)

**Title:** Why does your voice sound different on a recording?
**Setup:** You know how you sound. Then you play it back.

**Paragraph:**
[1] Play a recording of yourself and it feels wrong: that doesn't sound like me. But that's the way your voice reaches everyone else, and always has. So which one is the odd one out, the recording or the voice you hear when you talk? [2] When you talk, your voice reaches your ears two ways. Some goes out through the air and into your ears, like any sound. The rest travels as vibrations inside your head, through bone and flesh. Your friends only get the air route. [3] The inside route carries the low, deep part of a voice well, so it makes your own voice fuller. Put your fingers in your ears and talk: your voice turns boomy, because blocking the air route leaves the inside one louder. [4] A microphone sits outside your head, so it only picks up the air route, the same one your friends get. So the recording isn't the odd one out. Your live voice is, because only you get the extra route through your head.

**Bold line:** Everyone hears your voice through the air. Only you hear it through your head too.

**Go deeper:** Bone-conduction headphones play sound through the bones of your skull to your inner ear, which leaves your ears free to hear the room. It's the same inside route your own voice uses, now used on purpose.

**Slide most likely to lose a reader:** [3]. "The low, deep part of a voice" asks the reader to hear a voice as two parts, and the fingers-in-ears test works only if they try it. The picture should split a voice into a low part and a high part and show the inside route passing the low part. Runner-up: [1], which asks "which one is the odd one out" before there are two routes; [4] now answers it in plain words.

**What I kept out on purpose:**
- "Higher" or "deeper pitch". The facts sheet marks it contested: a 2025 abstract found most people thought their recording sounded lower. The paragraph says "fuller" only, never higher or lower.
- "The recording is exactly what others hear." Microphone placement and playback colour it, so the paragraph claims only the route: [4] says the microphone gets "the same one your friends get". Fixed at the Stage 4 recheck: [1] used to say the recording is "the voice everyone else has heard"; it now says "the way your voice reaches everyone else".
- Familiarity and the attractiveness study (claim 9) and brain suppression (claim 10). Both are real, but they are only part of the reason and the copy would need hedges. If the owner wants a "why does it feel so bad" beat, claim 9 is the one to add, as a fifth slide.
