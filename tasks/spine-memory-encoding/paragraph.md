# memory-encoding (owner approved 2026-11-09)

**Title:** How does your brain decide what to remember?
**Setup:** The first of three memory steps, told on its own: how something gets into memory at all.

**Spoken paragraph:**
[1] Ever forgotten where you put your keys? Odds are, you never really “lost” that memory. You never made it. Making a memory starts with encoding: turning what you see and hear into a form your brain can keep. [2] Your senses take in far more than you could ever keep, so attention works like a spotlight. Only what it lands on gets a chance to become a memory; the rest mostly fades. [3] What you noticed then waits in working memory, a tiny workspace that holds about four things. Once your mind moves on, they slip away in seconds, unless you work with them. [4] And how you work with them decides how well they stick. Just repeating a phone number leaves a weak memory, but thinking about what something means, and linking it to what you know, leaves a strong one. [5] You can picture a penny, but could you draw which way the head faces, or where the words sit? Most people can't: they've seen thousands but never really looked. You can't remember what you never took in.
