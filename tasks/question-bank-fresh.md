# Question bank, round 3: fresh ideas from an unbiased agent

2026-10-26. A fresh subagent got only the app's purpose and users (no earlier banks, no taste notes) and proposed 60 questions. Its words, lightly formatted. "(check)" means the agent flagged the fact as contested.

## Body
- Why do your fingers wrinkle in the bath? — Nerves squeeze blood vessels on purpose; numbed fingers don't wrinkle. (check: the "better grip" reason is debated)
- Why do you get goosebumps when you're cold or scared? — Tiny muscles try to fluff up fur we no longer have.
- Where does the fat go when you lose weight? — Most of it is breathed out as carbon dioxide.
- Why does your voice sound weird on a recording? — You usually hear yourself through your skull bones too.

## Food
- Why does popcorn pop? — Water inside the kernel turns to steam until the shell bursts.
- Why do onions make you cry? — Cutting mixes two chemicals that make a gas, which drifts to your eyes.
- How does bread rise? — Yeast eats sugar and gives off gas. Stretchy gluten traps the bubbles.
- Why does chilli feel hot when it isn't? — The chilli chemical trips the same nerve switch that real heat does.

## Home
- How does a fridge make things cold? — It pumps heat out, which is why the back is warm.
- How does a microwave heat food? — It shakes water molecules. It does not cook from the inside out.
- How does a toilet flush without a pump? — Many toilets use a siphon: the water pulls itself out. (check: some designs just wash down)
- How does a lock know it's your key? — Your key lifts the pins to exactly one line.

## Phone and internet
- How does a message reach a phone across the world? — It goes through cables on the sea floor, not satellites.
- How does a touchscreen know where your finger is? — Your finger disturbs a grid of electric charge. That's why gloves don't work.
- How does a torn QR code still work? — The same data is stored more than once so missing parts can be rebuilt.

## Money and shopping
- Where does the money come from when a bank gives a loan? — Mostly the bank creates new money by typing it in. (check: needs careful wording)
- How does a barcode tell the till the price? — The barcode is only a number. The price lives in the shop's computer.

## Plants and nature
- Where does a tree get all its weight from? — Mostly from the air. The tree is built from carbon dioxide, not soil.
- Why do leaves change colour in autumn? — The yellow was there all along. The green fades away.
- How does a seed know which way is up? — Tiny grains settle under gravity inside the root cells. (check)

## Animals
- How does a falling cat land on its feet? — It twists its front half, then its back half, with nothing to push off.
- How do bees tell each other where flowers are? — A dance where the angle means the direction compared to the sun.
- How can a gecko walk up a glass wall? — Billions of tiny hairs on its feet grip by molecular attraction.
- How do birds find their way south every winter? — They use the sun, the stars and Earth's magnetic field. (check)

## Weather and Earth
- Why is the sea salty but rivers aren't? — Rivers carry a tiny bit of salt in. The water evaporates and the salt stays.
- How does a cloud float if it's made of water? — A cloud weighs tonnes, but its tiny drops fall very slowly and rising air holds them up.
- What makes the sound of thunder? — Lightning heats the air hotter than the Sun's surface, and the air bursts outward.
- Why is it colder on a mountain, closer to the Sun? — Rising air spreads out and cools. Distance to the Sun doesn't matter.

## Space
- Why do we always see the same side of the Moon? — The Moon spins exactly once for each trip around Earth.
- Why do we have seasons? — Earth's tilt, not distance. Earth is closest to the Sun in January.
- Why do stars twinkle? — Moving air bends starlight. Planets twinkle less because they look like tiny discs.
- Why does the Moon look huge near the horizon? — It's the same size. Your brain is fooled. (check: the cause is debated)

## Cities
- How do traffic lights know a car is waiting? — A loop of wire under the road senses the metal of the car.
- Where does the water go after you flush? — Through sewers to a plant where bacteria eat the waste.
- How does water reach the top floor of a tall building? — Pumps fill tanks on the roof, then gravity does the rest.
- Why are manhole covers round? — A circle can't fall through its own hole. (check: one of several reasons)

## Transport
- How does a plane stay up in the air? — The wing pushes air down, so the air pushes the wing up. (check: avoid the "equal transit" myth)
- How does a steel ship weighing thousands of tonnes float? — It pushes aside water that weighs as much as the ship.
- How do trains turn without a steering wheel? — The wheels are slightly cone-shaped, so they centre themselves on the rails.
- Why do planes leave white lines in the sky? — Water in the engine exhaust freezes into ice clouds.

## Sport
- How does a footballer make the ball curve? — Spin makes the air push the ball sideways.
- Why do golf balls have dimples? — Dimples cut air drag, so the ball flies much further.
- Why do cyclists ride in a tight line? — The riders behind push through much less air.

## Music and sound
- Why does a guitar string make different notes? — A shorter, tighter or thinner string shakes faster and sounds higher.
- How does a speaker turn electricity into sound? — A magnet and a coil push a cone back and forth.
- Why do you hear the sea in a seashell? — It's room noise bouncing around inside, not the sea.

## Language
- Why does English spell words so strangely? — Spelling froze when printing came, but the way people spoke kept changing. (check)
- Why does a word sound fake if you repeat it? — Your brain gets tired of linking the sound to its meaning.

## History
- How did people tell the time before clocks? — Shadows, dripping water, burning candles.
- How did sailors find their way before GPS? — Stars gave latitude. Longitude needed an accurate clock.
- Why does a week have seven days? — Probably from the seven moving lights ancient people saw in the sky. (check)
- Why does every country have time zones? — Railways forced towns to stop keeping their own local noon.

## Buildings
- How do skyscrapers not blow over? — They sway on purpose. Some have giant swinging weights inside.
- Why do bridges have gaps in them? — Metal grows in summer heat and needs room.
- How do stone arches stay up without glue? — Each stone pushes on the stones next to it, all the way down.

## Everyday objects
- How does a zip stay shut? — The slider wedges the teeth together so they hook into each other.
- Why does a mirror flip left and right but not up and down? — It doesn't flip left and right. It flips front to back.

## The mind
- Why can't you tickle yourself? — Your brain predicts your own touch and turns it down.
- Why do you see spots after looking at a bright light? — The cells in your eye that saw it are worn out for a moment.

## Health
- How does a painkiller know where it hurts? — It doesn't. It goes all through your body.
