# spicy-sweat (working file; owner approved the explanation, no further gate)

**Title (owner's words):** Why do you start sweating when you eat spicy food?
**Setup:** The title says the whole puzzle: spicy food, sweat. Card 1 explains what "spicy" is.

**Spoken paragraph (cut):**
The spicy part of a chili pepper is a compound called capsaicin. [1] It switches on a heat sensor on your nerve endings, one that normally fires only for something truly hot, above about 43°C. So your brain hears "burning hot", even though nothing is. [2] Your body answers as if it were overheating. Blood vessels widen, your heart may beat faster, and your sweat glands start up to cool you down, mostly on your forehead, scalp, nose and upper lip. [3] Spicy food can make your nose run and your eyes water too: the same reaction to the same message. [4] People who eat spicy food a lot get used to it, because their heat sensors become less reactive over time.
