# Question bank (candidates, questions only)

Raw candidates for new topics, 2026-10-26. No copy yet. The owner picks, then each pick goes through `write-explainer` (and a title truth check, a cold reader and a fact checker).

**Screen used on every line:** a plain question a 10-year-old could ask, surprising enough that the title carries it, picturable as a tiny animation, and more than a one-line answer. Format: question, then the surprise or picture idea (12 words or fewer).

**Watch-outs for the picker**

- Several lines have a contested or nuanced fact (marked "check" in the note). Run the fact check before writing.

## Space
- Why do astronauts float on the space station when gravity is still pulling them? — They fall all the time and keep missing Earth.
- Why does a rocket spend most of its effort going sideways, not up? — Orbit needs sideways speed, not height. Cannonball picture.
- Why is a day on Venus longer than its year? — Spin slower than orbit. Two clocks, one planet.
- Why does the Moon always show us the same face? — It does spin: once per orbit. Marked spot.
- Why do stars twinkle but planets mostly don't? — Pinpoint light versus a tiny disc, through moving air.
- How do we measure how far away a star is? — Thumb trick: the star shifts as Earth moves.

## Biology
- How does a seed know which way is up? — Heavy grains inside cells sink and tell the root.
- Why doesn't a spider get stuck in its own web? — Sticky threads and plain threads. It walks the plain ones.
- How does a tall tree lift water 100 metres with no pump? — Evaporation pulls a chain of water up. Check.
- Where does the weight go when you lose fat? — Mostly breathed out as a gas. Follow the atoms.
- Does a caterpillar really melt inside its cocoon? — Mostly yes. Old body dissolves, tiny seeds rebuild it.
- How does a mosquito find you in the dark? — Breath gas, warmth, smell. A trail to your skin.

## Machines
- Does a microwave really cook food from the inside out? — No. Waves reach only a short way in.
- How does a zipper lock with one pull? — A tiny wedge squeezes each tooth into the next.
- How does a lock know which key is the right one? — Pins lift to one line. Show a wrong key failing.
- How does a toilet empty itself with one push? — A hidden bent pipe starts a self-pulling flow (siphon).
- Where does the heat from your food go when a fridge cools it? — Out the back, into your kitchen. Heat moved, not cold made.
- What stops a lift falling if its cable snaps? — Spring brakes grip the rails when the speed is wrong.

## Nature
- Why do birds fly in a V? — Each bird rides the lifting air of the one ahead.
- How does a Venus flytrap count to two? — It shuts only after two touches close together.
- How do bees tell each other where the flowers are? — A wiggle dance: angle is direction, length is distance.
- How do ants find the shortest path with no leader? — Scent trails get stronger on the quick route.
- Why do leaves turn orange in autumn? — The orange was always there, hidden by green.
- How do salmon find the river where they were born? — They follow its smell across an ocean. Check.

## Internet
- How does a message cross an ocean in a blink? — Mostly along cables on the sea floor.
- How does a website know you are the same person from one click to the next? — A small ticket kept in your browser.
- How does a whole film fit through your phone? — It sends only what changes between pictures.
- How does a search engine look through billions of pages in a blink? — It reads a book index, not the pages.
- Where are your photos when they are "in the cloud"? — Buildings full of disks. Walk along a rack.
- What happens in the second after you type a web address? — A phone book lookup, then a hop across many computers.

## Money
- Where is your money when it sits in the bank? — Mostly lent to other people. Follow one coin.
- Who decides what a share of a company costs? — Buyers and sellers line up, and the price is where they meet.
- Why does a small pile of money grow slowly, then suddenly? — Interest earns interest. Doubling squares on a board.
- How does a vending machine know a coin is real? — Weight, size and magnet tests, one gate each.
- Where does money come from when a bank gives you a loan? — Mostly typed into being. Ledger picture needs testing.
- Why does a dollar buy less each year? — Same basket, rising price tags. Basket shrinks.

## Mind
- Why can't you tickle yourself? — Your brain predicts your own touch and turns it down.
- Why can't you see the hole in your eyesight? — Your brain fills the gap. Dot test.
- Why can a rubber hand start to feel like yours? — Brush both together and your brain moves your hand.
- How does a magician fool you right in front of your eyes? — You miss what you do not watch. Hidden gorilla idea.
- Why can you remember something wrongly and feel sure? — Each memory is rebuilt, and can change. Telephone-game picture.
- Why do you see faces in clouds and sockets? — Your brain finds faces first. Spot them in random shapes.

## Physics
- Would a feather and a hammer really fall together on the Moon? — Yes, with no air. Side-by-side drop.
- Why does a spinning top stay up when it would fall if still? — Spin resists tipping. Push it and watch it circle.
- Why does a mirror seem to swap left and right but not up and down? — It swaps front and back. Walk through it.
- Why does a soap bubble show rainbow colours? — A film thinner than light bounces waves apart.
- Why does metal feel colder than wood in the same room? — Same temperature. Metal pulls heat from you faster.
- Why does a pendulum take about the same time for big and small swings? — Longer path, faster speed. Two swings race.

## Chemistry
- How does salt make ice melt? — It makes freezing harder, so ice turns back to water.
- How does a glow stick glow with no battery? — Snapping a tube mixes two liquids that give off light.
- Why does a shaken fizzy drink explode? — Gas hides in the liquid. Shaking makes bubble seeds.
- Is fire a solid, a liquid or a gas? — A glowing gas, made by the reaction. Zoom in.
- How does a firework get its colours? — Each metal burns its own colour. Match metal to colour.
- How does soap clean what water cannot? — One end grabs water, one end grabs grease. Lifts it off.

## Earth
- Is the ground under your feet moving? — Yes, about as fast as fingernails grow. Rewind a map.
- Why is the sea salty but a river is not? — Rivers carry tiny amounts of salt. Sea water leaves it behind.
- How do we know what is inside the Earth when nobody can dig that deep? — Earthquake waves bend and stop. Read the echoes.
- Why is it colder at the top of a mountain, closer to the Sun? — Air cools as it spreads out. The sun heats the ground.
- How does a cloud stay up when water is heavy? — Droplets are tiny and rising air holds them.
- Where do waves come from before they reach a calm beach? — Storms thousands of miles away. Follow a wave back.

## History
- How did a Greek man measure the whole Earth with a stick? — Two shadows, two towns, one angle.
- How did Roman water flow into cities with no pumps? — A gentle downhill slope all the way, for many miles.
- How did the Romans build roads that still exist? — Cross-section: layers of stone, gravel and sand.
- How did sailors know where they were before satellites? — Star angle and a clock. Check.
- How did a printing press copy a page in minutes? — Reusable metal letters, inked and pressed. Contrast with hand copying.
- How did people tell the time before clocks? — A moving shadow. Check how they found noon.

## Music
- Why do some notes sound good together and others clash? — Their waves line up every few beats, or never.
- Why does a violin sound different from a flute on the same note? — Hidden extra notes mix in differently. Wave shapes.
- How does a record turn a groove into music? — The needle wiggles in the groove. Zoom in to bumps.
- How do noise-cancelling headphones make noise disappear? — They add the opposite wave. Two waves add to nothing.
- Why does your voice sound different on a recording? — You hear yourself through your bones too.
- Why does a train horn drop in pitch as it passes? — Waves bunch up in front and stretch behind.

## Food
- Why does popcorn pop? — Steam trapped in a hard shell bursts it. Slow motion.
- Why does an egg go solid when you cook it? — Coiled proteins unfold and tangle. Watch the strands.
- Why does chilli feel hot when it is not? — It tricks the sensor that warns of heat.
- Why does honey never go off? — Too little water for germs. Show a germ drying out.
- Why do onions make you cry? — Cutting makes a gas that stings your eyes.
- Why does toast taste so different from bread? — Browning builds new flavour pieces. Zoom on the surface.
- Why does a pressure cooker cook faster? — Water boils hotter when squeezed. Check.

## Medicine
- How can a harmless jab protect you from a disease? — Your body practises on a fake. Practice fight.
- How does a painkiller know where it hurts? — It does not. It travels everywhere.
- Why does a cut stop bleeding by itself? — Tiny blood bits make a net. Watch it weave.
- Why do you get a fever when you are ill? — Heat slows germs and speeds the defenders. Check.
- How does an X-ray see your bones through your skin? — Bone blocks the rays more. Shadow picture.
- Why does a bruise change colour from purple to yellow? — Spilled blood breaks down in stages.

## AI
- How does a computer tell a cat from a dog? — Many simple filters, each spotting one clue.
- How does a chatbot choose its next word? — It guesses the most likely word, one at a time.
- How does a computer learn from its mistakes? — Nudge many knobs until the error shrinks. Knob picture.
- How does an AI draw a picture from words? — It starts from noise and clears it away. Noise to picture.
- Why do chatbots sometimes make things up? — They guess likely words, not look up facts.
- How does a self-driving car see the road? — Lasers measure a 3D map many times a second.

## Security
- How can two strangers share a secret in front of everyone? — Paint-mixing trick: easy to mix, hard to unmix.
- How can a website check your password without knowing it? — It keeps a scrambled version that cannot be unscrambled.
- How long would a thief take to guess your password? — Count the tries. Length beats cleverness.
- How does a hotel key card open only your room? — A code on the card is checked and expires.
- How can you tell a real banknote from a perfect copy? — Hidden features. Tilt it and watch them change.
- What does the little padlock in your browser do? — Locks the line so others cannot read it.

## Flight
- How does a plane hold itself up? — The wing throws air downward. Streamlines bending.
- Why do planes leave white lines in the sky? — Warm engine air freezes into cloud. Check.
- Why do plane windows have a tiny hole? — It protects the glass and lets pressure spread.
- Why does a helicopter need a small propeller on its tail? — Without it the body would spin the other way.
- Why is the flight from New York to London faster than the way back? — A river of fast air high up.
- How can a plane land in thick fog? — Radio beams draw an invisible slope to the runway.

## Energy
- Why don't birds get zapped on a power line? — Both feet at the same voltage, so no path.
- Why do power lines carry thousands of volts when your home uses far less? — High voltage wastes less on the way. Transformer steps.
- How does a solar panel make electricity with nothing moving? — Light knocks electrons loose, and they flow.
- When you flip a switch, how does the light come on at once? — Electrons crawl, but the push travels fast.
- What actually runs out when a battery goes flat? — Its ingredients are used up. Show them depleting.
- How can a lake be a battery? — Pump water uphill when power is cheap, then let it fall.

## Cities
- How does a skyscraper stop swaying in the wind? — A giant weight in the top swings the other way.
- How does a traffic light know a car is waiting? — A wire loop under the road feels the metal.
- How does water reach the top floor of a tall building? — Pumps, tanks, and the weight of the water.
- Why is a city hotter than the countryside around it? — Dark roofs and roads soak up and keep heat.
- Where does the water go when you flush? — Down pipes to a plant that cleans it.
- How do you dig a tunnel under a river? — A giant drill with a ring wall built behind it.
- How does a big city get fed every day? — Only a few days of food. Trucks flow in like blood.

## Math
- How many people must be in a room for two to share a birthday? — Just 23. Draw lines between every pair.
- How many times must you fold a paper to reach the Moon? — About 42 times if it could be done. Doubling.
- What happens if you cut a twisted paper loop down the middle? — One long loop, not two. Cut it live.
- Why does switching doors double your chance in the three-door game? — The host's reveal leaks information. Replay it 100 times.
- Why do sunflower seeds spiral in two directions at once? — Each new seed turns the same small angle. Seed packing.
- Why is a billion so much bigger than a million? — A million seconds is days, a billion is decades. Clock strip.

## Art
- How do three colours of ink make every colour in a magazine? — Zoom in: tiny dots your eye blends.
- Why do the eyes in a painting seem to follow you? — A flat picture looks the same from every side.
- Why does mixing paints make brown but mixing lights make white? — Paint takes colour away, light adds it. Two mixing rigs.
- How can a flat drawing look deep? — Lines meet at one distant point. Draw a road.
- How do thirty still pictures a second become a movie? — Your eye holds each one briefly. Flip-book picture.
- Why does the same grey look lighter on a dark background? — Your eye compares. Two identical squares.

## Language
- Why do so many languages call mum "mama"? — Babies' first easy sounds became the words. Map of languages.
- How did anyone read a language nobody could read? — One stone with the same text in three scripts.
- Why does a foreign language sound like one long blur? — Speech has no gaps between words. Waveform picture.
- Why do we write letters we never say, like the k in "knight"? — We stopped saying them but kept the spelling. Timeline.
- Can a brand-new language appear in one generation? — Children invented one with no teacher. Check.
- How can people talk across a valley by whistling? — Whistles copy the speech tune. Wave on a map.

## Law
- How does a hair point to one person out of billions? — Match several markers. Fits like a bar code.
- Why do some countries drive on the left? — Horse and sword habits. Check, and the story varies.
- Who owns the air above your house? — Layers: your land, planes' sky, space. Zoom upward.
- How does a speed camera know how fast you went? — Two lines in the road, or a radar echo.
- Can a lie detector really tell when you are lying? — It senses nerves, not lies. Graph of the same reaction.

## Sport
- How can a footballer bend a free kick around a wall? — Spin makes the air push the ball sideways.
- Why can a cyclist ride faster behind another? — A pocket of calm air, no head wind.
- Why does a skater spin faster when pulling in their arms? — Same spin, smaller circle. Arms in, spin up.
- Why do golf balls have dimples? — Dimples help air stick, so the ball goes further.
- How can a high jumper clear a bar their body's middle goes under? — Arched back lets the middle stay below the bar. Check.
- How can a sailboat sail against the wind? — The sail works like a wing. Zigzag path.
