# Fact sheet: How do you play poker?

## 1. Is the title literally true?
Yes, as long as the copy says which poker it teaches. Poker is a family of card games, and the rules differ between them. This topic explains Texas Hold'em, the kind that took over from seven-card stud as the most common game in US casinos, and the one played in the World Series of Poker Main Event. The copy must name it once ("the most common kind, Texas Hold'em") and must not claim these rules apply to every poker game. Also, the topic shows how a hand works, not how to play well; it must not promise strategy.

## 2. The answer in one sentence
Everyone gets two secret cards, five shared cards are turned face up in the middle in three steps, players bet before and after each step, and the hand is won either by the last player left when everyone else gives up, or, if cards are shown, by whoever can make the best five-card hand.

## 3. Safe claims
1. Texas Hold'em uses one ordinary pack of 52 cards, with no jokers.
   *high — https://homepokertourney.org/roberts-rules-of-poker-version-6.htm (Robert's Rules of Poker, Bob Ciaffone; same text at https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf)*
2. Each player is dealt two cards face down that only they can look at.
   *high — https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (Section 5, Hold'em: "two downcards as their personal hand")*
3. Five more cards are turned face up in the middle of the table, and they belong to everyone: every player can use them.
   *high — https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (glossary: community cards "can be used by all players")*
4. The five shared cards come out in three steps: three at once (the flop), then one more (the turn), then one last card (the river).
   *high — https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (Section 5; glossary: flop, turncard)*
5. There is a round of betting after the two secret cards are dealt, and another after each of the three steps, so four rounds in all.
   *high — https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (Section 5)*
6. Your hand is the best five cards you can pick from seven: your two secret cards and the five shared ones. You can use both of your cards, one, or even none.
   *high — https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (Section 5: "any five-card combination", "play the board")*
7. All the money or chips that players bet go into one pile in the middle, called the pot, and the winner of the hand takes it.
   *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_hold_%27em*
8. When it is your turn to bet, you can check (bet nothing and pass, only if nobody has bet yet), call (put in the same amount as the last bet), raise (put in more than the last bet, so others must match it), or fold (throw your cards away and give up the pot).
   *high — https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (glossary: check, raise, fold; rules on betting)*
9. Before any cards are dealt, two players must put in small bets, called the small blind and the big blind, so there is always something in the pot to win.
   *high — https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (glossary: "BLIND: A required bet made before any cards are dealt"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_hold_%27em*
10. If everyone else folds, the one player left wins the pot straight away and does not have to show their cards, so they may have been holding the worst cards at the table.
    *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_hold_%27em ("is not required to show their hole cards")*
11. If two or more players are still in after the last round of betting, they show their cards, and the best five-card hand takes the pot; if two hands are exactly equal, they share it.
    *high — https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (The Showdown; Ties); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_hold_%27em*
12. From weakest to strongest, the hands are: high card (nothing matches), one pair, two pairs, three of a kind, a straight (five cards in a row, like 5-6-7-8-9), a flush (five cards of one suit), a full house (three of a kind plus a pair), four of a kind, a straight flush (five in a row, all one suit), and the royal flush (10, jack, queen, king, ace, all one suit), which is the best possible hand.
    *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_poker_hands (order); https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (glossary: flush, straight, straight flush, full house)*
13. Suits do not make one hand better than another in Hold'em: a flush in hearts and the same flush in spades are equal.
    *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_poker_hands ("Suits are not ranked"); https://www.pagat.com/docs/RobsPkrRulesHome.pdf (Ties: "Suits never break a tie for winning a pot")*

Notes for the writer:
- Claim 12 counts the royal flush as its own step for clarity. Strictly, it is just the highest straight flush. Listing it separately is fine; saying it is a "different kind of hand" is not needed.
- Claim 8: "check" is only allowed when nobody has bet in that round yet. If the copy uses check, keep that condition or leave check out.
- Claim 10 is the planned surprise and it is fully true. Say "may have had the worst cards", not "usually has bad cards".

## 4. Myths to avoid
- **"You need the best cards to win."** False. A hand often ends when everyone else folds, and then the last player wins without showing anything. Best cards only matter when cards are shown.
- **"The cards in the middle are the dealer's hand."** False in Hold'em. They are shared by every player; the dealer does not play against you (that is blackjack).
- **"You must use both of your own cards."** False. You use the best five of your seven, which can mean one of yours or none.
- **"Spades beat hearts" (suits rank).** Not in Hold'em when deciding who wins the pot. Equal hands share the pot. (Some card rooms use suit order only for side jobs like picking seats.)
- **"Royal flushes happen all the time."** They are extremely rare (see the number below). TV makes big hands look common because only the exciting ones are shown.
- **"Bluffing is cheating."** False. Betting as if you have good cards when you don't is a normal, allowed part of the game. What is against the rules is things like marking cards or secretly teaming up.
- **"Poker is the game with seven cards dealt to you."** That is seven-card stud, a different poker game. In Hold'em you only ever get two cards of your own.
- **"Whoever bets the most wins."** No. Money decides nothing at the showdown; only the cards do.

## 5. One good number
In Texas Hold'em, the chance of a player ending a hand with a royal flush among their seven cards is about 1 in 31,000. If you played one hand every day, you would expect to wait about 85 years for one.
*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poker_probability (7-card royal flush: 4,324 of 133,784,560 hands, odds 30,939 to 1). Solid: it is exact counting, not a survey. Caveat for the writer: this counts staying in to see all seven cards. In five-card poker (just five cards dealt to you) it is far rarer, about 1 in 650,000. Use one figure only, and say "in Texas Hold'em".*

Rejected: "how often hands end without a showdown". Online sites and trackers quote figures, but they vary hugely with the game and players, and no rule body or reliable study publishes one. Do not use a number for this.

## 6. Everyday comparisons
- **A salad bar.** Everyone has their own two items on their plate and shares the same five dishes on the bar, and each person picks their best five-item meal. Right intuition for shared cards and "best five of seven". Misleads only if it suggests you take cards away from the middle; the shared cards stay for everyone. Usable with that line.
- **Rock-paper-scissors with a poker face.** Rejected: suggests the result is pure chance and simultaneous; poker has rounds and choices.
- **A staring contest / game of chicken.** Captures the fold surprise: the last one who doesn't back down wins, whatever they hold. Right intuition for winning without showing. Misleads if read as "the boldest player always wins"; at a showdown only the cards count. Use only for the fold moment, not the whole game.
