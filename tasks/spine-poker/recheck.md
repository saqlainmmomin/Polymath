# Poker: fact recheck of slides.md against facts.md

Three flags. Everything else matches the sheet. The hand order, the three-one-one deal, the four betting rounds, best five of seven, the fold win, the showdown, the royal flush, the Go deeper bluffing line, and the pair vs two pairs picture (two pairs win, correctly) are all fine.

1. **Slides 1 and 4, picture and label: the 2 and 7 shown as fact to be "the worst cards at the table".** The picture of slide 1 says the cards turn over to show "a 2 and a 7, the worst cards at the table", and the label says "THE WORST CARDS". The other players' cards are never shown, so the picture can't prove the 2 and 7 were the worst. A 2 and 7 of one suit would not be the weakest pair of cards either. The sheet only goes as far as "may": "they may have been holding the worst cards at the table." (claim 10; writer note: "may have had the worst cards"). At the least, the 2 and 7 must be drawn in two different suits.

2. **Slide 3 headline: "Everyone bets that their hand will win".** A reader takes this as what a bet means. But players fold, and they also bet without expecting to win (the bluffing in Go deeper; slide 1's worst-cards win). The sheet's sentence is: "All the money or chips that players bet go into one pile in the middle, called the pot, and the winner of the hand takes it." (claim 7).

3. **Slide 5: "Playing a round a day, you'd wait about 85 years for one."** The sheet requires this figure to name the game, because it is far rarer in other kinds of poker. The sheet's sentence: "In Texas Hold'em, the chance of a player ending a hand with a royal flush among their seven cards is about 1 in 31,000. If you played one hand every day, you would expect to wait about 85 years for one." (section 5: "say 'in Texas Hold'em'").
