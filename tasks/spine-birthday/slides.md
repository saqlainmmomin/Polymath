## Slides

### Slide 1
**Headline:** In a room of 23, what's the chance two share a birthday?
**Setup:** Take a guess before you read on.
**Text:** Many people guess a small chance, but it's about 50%. A true fact that sounds wrong is called a paradox, and this one is the birthday paradox.
**Hook:** So why does 50% feel so wrong?
**Picture:** A room of 23 hand-drawn people. A dial labelled CHANCE first points low (YOUR GUESS), then swings up to the middle (ABOUT 50%). Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** YOUR GUESS · ABOUT 50% · 23 PEOPLE

### Slide 2
**Headline:** It feels wrong because we think of our own birthday
**Text:** Walk into a room of 22 strangers, and the chance someone shares your birthday is less than 6%.
**Hook:** But does the match have to include you?
**Picture:** You, drawn in gold, with your day marked on a calendar. 22 strangers drop their birthdays onto the calendar one by one; none land on your day, which is what happens in about 94 rooms in 100. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** YOU · YOUR BIRTHDAY · 22 STRANGERS · LESS THAN 6%

### Slide 3
**Headline:** The match doesn't have to include you
**Text:** If all 23 of you shook hands once with each other, there'd be 253 handshakes. Each handshake is a pair who could share a birthday, and only 22 of them include you.
**Hook:** So what do all those pairs do to the chance?
**Picture:** 23 people in a ring. Handshake lines appear one by one; your 22 are gold, the other 231 ink, until the ring is full. A counter climbs to 253. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** 253 HANDSHAKES · 22 INCLUDE YOU · ONE LINE = ONE PAIR

### Slide 4
**Headline:** More pairs means a higher chance of a match
**Text:** Any one pair almost never matches. But 253 pairs push the chance of a match to about 50%.
**Hook:** So what happens as more people walk in?
**Picture:** Room after room of 23 is dealt onto a calendar. Rooms with a match get a tick, rooms without a cross; the tally settles near half. Loops by itself. (Never show "1 in 365" next to 253: readers divide.)
**Picture labels:** ROOMS WITH A MATCH · ROOMS WITHOUT · ABOUT 50%

### Slide 5
**Headline:** Pairs pile up fast
**Text:** Each new person makes a new pair with everyone already in the room. By 57 people there are 1,596 pairs, and the chance of a match is over 99%.
**Bold:** Count the pairs, not the people.
**Picture:** People walk into the room one at a time. Two counters, PEOPLE and PAIRS, and a CHANCE bar climb together; at 57 the bar is nearly full. The topic's one hands-on control: drag to add people.
**Picture labels:** PEOPLE · PAIRS · CHANCE OF A MATCH

### Go deeper
At the 2014 football World Cup, each squad had 23 players, and about half the squads had two players with the same birthday. To get a 50% chance that a stranger shares your birthday, you'd need 253 strangers, the same number as the pairs in a room of 23. Real rooms match a little more often, because births aren't spread evenly across the year.
