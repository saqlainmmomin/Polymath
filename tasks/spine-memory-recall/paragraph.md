# memory-recall (owner approved 2026-11-09)

**Title:** How does your brain bring back a memory?
**Setup:** The third of three memory steps, told on its own: what happens when you remember.

**Spoken paragraph:**
[1] Remembering isn't pressing play on a video. A memory is kept as pieces, what you saw, heard and felt, stored in different parts of your brain and linked together. [2] Recall starts with a cue: a question, a smell, a song or a place that matches one of those pieces. [3] That piece wakes up, and the signal spreads along the links until the whole memory comes back. When nothing comes, the memory is often still there, missing only the right cue: that's the tip-of-the-tongue feeling. [4] And because the pieces are put back together each time, your brain fills any gaps with what it knows and expects, so a memory can change without you noticing. [5] In one famous experiment, people watched a car crash. Those asked how fast the cars were going when they "smashed" guessed higher speeds than those asked about cars that "hit". Some even remembered broken glass that was never there. Remembering is rebuilding, not replaying.
