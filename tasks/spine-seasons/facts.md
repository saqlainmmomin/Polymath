# Fact sheet: Why is it summer when Earth is farthest from the Sun?

## 1. Is the title literally true?
Yes, for the north half of Earth. Earth is farthest from the Sun in early July, in the middle of summer for Europe, India and the USA. For Australia it is winter then, so the copy must name the north half (or its countries) when it says "summer in July".

## 2. The answer in one sentence
Earth leans, so for half the year your half of Earth leans toward the Sun and gets less slanted sunlight for more hours a day, and that is summer.

## 3. Safe claims
1. Earth goes round the Sun once a year on a path that is very nearly a circle.
   *high — https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html*
2. Earth is nearest the Sun in early January and farthest in early July.
   *high — https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en/*
3. In July Earth is only about 3 percent farther from the Sun than in January (about 152 against 147 million km).
   *high — https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html*
4. The north and south halves of Earth have opposite seasons at the same moment: when Australia has summer, Canada has winter, at the same distance from the Sun.
   *high — https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/earth-system/seasons*
5. Earth leans about 23 degrees from upright as it goes round the Sun.
   *high — https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html (obliquity 23.44)*
6. The lean keeps pointing the same way in space all year, toward the same faraway spot.
   *high — https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en/*
7. So in June the north half leans toward the Sun, and in December it leans away; the south half does the opposite.
   *high — https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en/*
8. Light that hits a surface straight on lands on a small patch; the same light coming in at a slant spreads over more ground, so each bit of ground gets less.
   *high — https://www.weather.gov/fsd/season*
9. The half leaning toward the Sun gets its sunlight less slanted, so each bit of ground there gets more light and warms more.
   *high — https://www.weather.gov/fsd/season*
10. Leaning toward the Sun also puts more of your half on the sunny side of Earth as it spins, so your days are longer and nights shorter, and the ground gets more hours of sunlight.
    *high — https://www.weather.gov/fsd/season*
11. In London, the longest June day has about 16 and a half hours of daylight and the shortest December day under 8, so about twice as long.
    *high — https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/uk/london*
12. The Sun is ever straight overhead only in the tropics; in London, Paris or New York it never gets there, even in June.
    *high — https://www.britannica.com/place/Tropic-of-Cancer*

## 4. Myths to avoid
- **"Summer is when Earth is closer to the Sun."** The north half has summer when Earth is farthest away.
- **"The half leaning toward the Sun is closer, so it's warmer."** The lean moves a place a few thousand km at most, nothing next to 150 million km. Warmth comes from the slant and the day length, not from getting closer.
- **"In summer the Sun is straight overhead."** Only in the tropics. Elsewhere it is higher at noon than in winter, but never straight up. Say "less slanted" or "more like the straight beam", never "straight down" for ordinary places.
- **"Higher sunlight."** Not wrong, but the owner found it meaningless. Use the torch picture instead.
- **"Earth rocks its lean back and forth over the year."** The lean stays pointed the same way all year; only Earth's place on its path changes.
- **"Distance has no effect at all."** It has a small one (about 7 percent more sunlight in January). Say distance "hardly changes", not that it does nothing.

## 5. One good number
Earth is only about 3 percent farther from the Sun in July than in January. If the Sun were 100 steps away, that is about 3 steps more.
*Source: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html (perihelion 147.1, aphelion 152.1 million km)*

## 6. Everyday comparisons
- **A torch on a table.** Straight down makes a small, bright spot; tilted, the same light spreads into a long, dim patch. Right intuition: same light, more ground, less warming per bit. Misleads only if read as "summer Sun is straight down", so the copy says "more like the straight beam".
- **A classroom globe on its stand.** Real classroom globes lean about 23 degrees, the same as Earth. Right intuition for the lean itself. Misleads if the reader pictures the globe turning to face the lamp, so the copy must say the lean keeps pointing the same way all year.
- **Leaning toward a campfire.** Rejected: you get warmer because you get closer, which is exactly the myth this topic overturns.

## 7. Go deeper thread
At the North Pole, the Sun stays up for about six months, then come months of twilight and dark. The same lean does it: for half the year the whole top of Earth leans into the light, and for the other half it leans out.
*high — https://www.timeanddate.com/astronomy/midnight-sun.html (round-one checker: say "about six months, then months of twilight and dark", not "six up, six down")*
