# Card payment, stage 2 (paragraph for owner approval)

**Title:** Why isn't the shop paid the moment you tap your card?
**Setup:** You tap, it beeps, and you walk away.

**Paragraph:**
[1] Tapping your card feels like paying. But the card reader only sends a short message asking your bank if this card can pay. So where does that message go before it comes back with a yes? [2] The reader sends it to the shop's bank, where the shop keeps its money. That bank passes it to a card network, a company such as Visa or Mastercard that links banks. The network passes it to your bank, which answers yes or no, and the answer travels back the same way, in a couple of seconds. [3] Your bank checks that the card is real and that you have enough money or credit. If so, it takes that amount off what you can spend straight away, so you can't spend the same money twice. But the money hasn't reached the shop yet. The yes is your bank's promise to pay. [4] Moving money for every single tap would be slow and expensive. So banks add many payments up and move the money in totals. That's why the shop is usually paid a few working days after your tap.

**Bold line:** The tap gets a yes in seconds, and the money follows a few working days later.

**Go deeper:** When you tap, the shop gets a little less than you paid: your bank, the card network and the shop's bank each take a small fee out of the amount as the money moves back along the same four places, and the money that finally reaches the shop is what is left. The fee that goes to your bank is called the interchange fee.
