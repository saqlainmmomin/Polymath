## Slides

### Slide 1
**Headline:** Why isn't the shop paid the moment you tap your card?
**Setup:** You tap, it beeps, and you walk away.
**Text:** Tapping your card feels like paying. But the card reader only sends a short message asking your bank if this card can pay.
**Hook:** So where does that message go before it comes back with a yes?
**Picture:** A hand taps a card on a reader. A short message leaves it with a question mark on it. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** CARD READER · MESSAGE · CAN THIS CARD PAY?

### Slide 2
**Headline:** The message goes to the shop's bank, then a card network, then your bank
**Text:** The reader's message reaches the shop's bank. That bank passes it to a card network, a company such as Visa or Mastercard that links banks. The network passes it to your bank, which answers yes or no. The answer travels back the same way, in a couple of seconds.
**Hook:** So when your bank says yes, does the shop get the money?
**Picture:** Four places in a row: the card reader, the shop's bank, the card network and your bank. The message hops along them one by one, then a yes hops back the same way. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** CARD READER · SHOP'S BANK · CARD NETWORK · YOUR BANK · YES

### Slide 3
**Headline:** Your bank's yes is a promise to pay, and the money hasn't reached the shop yet
**Text:** Your bank checks that the card is real and that you have enough money or credit. If so, it takes that amount off what you can spend straight away. So you can't spend the same money twice. But the money hasn't reached the shop yet.
**Hook:** So why does the shop have to wait to be paid?
**Picture:** Your bank on the left with a bar for what you can spend. The yes arrives and the bar drops by the amount of the tap. On the right the shop's till stays empty. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** YOUR BANK · WHAT YOU CAN SPEND · YOUR BANK'S YES · THE SHOP

### Slide 4
**Headline:** Banks move money in totals, so the shop is usually paid a few working days later
**Text:** Banks don't move money for every single tap. They gather many payments and add them up, and the shop's bank is paid in totals. So the shop is usually paid a few working days after your tap.
**Bold:** The yes comes back in seconds, and the shop is usually paid a few working days later.
**Picture:** Many small taps are added up into totals. A few working days tick past, and only then do the totals move from the banks to the shop. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** MANY PAYMENTS · TOTALS · A FEW WORKING DAYS · THE SHOP

### Go deeper
The shop gets a little less than you paid, because a small fee is shared among your bank, the card network and the shop's bank. The fee that goes to your bank is called the interchange fee.
