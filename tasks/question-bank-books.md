# Question bank, round 2: topics taken from children's books

2026-10-26. Questions only, no copy. Same screen as `question-bank.md`. These come from what real children's "how things work" and "why" books, and kids' question shows, cover. None repeats `question-bank.md` or an existing topic.

- *The Way Things Work Now*, David Macaulay: [chapter list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Way_Things_Work)
- *How Everything Works* (Lonely Planet Kids): [topic list](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/63103155)

- [Bookroo "how things work" shelf](https://bookroo.com/books/topics/how-things-work): *Cathedral*, *Toilet*, *Mistakes That Worked*, *Pop!*, *Whoosh!*, *The Street Beneath My Feet*
- Kids' question shows: [But Why](https://www.vermontpublic.org/programs/why-podcast-curious-kids) and [Brains On!](https://podwise.ai/podcasts/488) episode titles

The books give a topic, not a question. The titles below are my wording and still need the usual truth check and fact check. A tag after each note names where the idea came from.

**Counts:** Space 4, Biology 7, Machines 9, Internet 3, Money 1, Mind 2, Physics 5, Chemistry 3, Earth 4, History 1, Music 1, Food 1, Energy 3, Cities 1, Art 1. **Total: 46.**

**Honest gap:** these books lean towards machines, body, nature and space. They gave me nothing for AI, Security, Flight, Math, Language, Law, Sport, Medicine or Nature. `question-bank.md` is the better source for those.

## Space
- Are the stars in a constellation really close together? — Seen from the side, they are wildly far apart. [But Why]
- Why does a spacecraft glow red-hot when it comes back to Earth? — Squeezed air heats up, not scraping. [How Everything Works]
- What would happen if you fell into a black hole? — Stretched like spaghetti. Picture the pull. [TFK Big Book of Why]
- How big is the universe, really? — Zoom out step by step. [Tell Me Why]

## Biology
- Where does a tree get its weight from? — Mostly from air. Follow the gas into wood. [How Everything Works]
- How does a jellyfish sting with no brain? — A tiny harpoon fires in a blink. [Brains On]
- Are birds really dinosaurs? — Morph a bird skeleton into a dinosaur's. [Brains On]
- Why do animals' eyes glow in the dark? — A mirror layer bounces light back. [TFK Big Book of Why]
- Do plants breathe? — Tiny mouths on leaves open and close. [But Why]
- How does an oyster make a pearl? — Layer on layer around a speck. [But Why]
- Why do your feet stink? — Germs eat your sweat. [Brains On]

## Machines
- Is a screw really just a ramp? — Unwrap the thread and a slope appears. [Way Things Work]
- How can one person lift a heavy load with a few ropes and wheels? — Count the ropes. [Way Things Work]
- How can a small push on a jack lift a car? — Squeezed liquid trades distance for force. [Way Things Work]
- How does a drone stay level in the wind? — Four fans, quick sensors, constant fixing. [How Everything Works]
- How does a scanner read a price from black and white stripes? — Stripes are a number the laser reads. [Best Ever Why]
- How does a kettle know when to switch itself off? — Steam flips a small metal switch. [Best Ever Why]
- Does a vacuum cleaner really suck dirt up? — Outside air pushes the dirt in. [Look Inside]
- How can glue stick to paper and then peel off clean? — Tiny sticky balls touch only a little. [Mistakes That Worked]
- How does a metal detector find a coin under sand? — Coil and magnet pick up a changing field. [Way Things Work]

## Internet
- How does a screen know where your finger is? — Your touch changes a grid's electricity. [Way Things Work]
- How can a song turn into a list of numbers? — Measure the wave thousands of times a second. [Way Things Work]
- How does a voice travel down a wire? — Sound shakes a disc that shakes a current. [Way Things Work]

## Money
- Why do supermarkets put milk and bread at the back? — A walk past everything else. Check. [Best Ever Why]

## Mind
- What is your brain doing while you sleep? — Night cycles, light and deep. [How Everything Works]
- Why do we like getting scared? — Fear with a safe exit feels good. [Brains On]

## Physics
- Why do wet fingers stick to metal in a freezer? — Water freezes between skin and metal. [TFK Big Book of Why]
- Is a rainbow really a circle? — The ground hides the bottom half. [Best Ever Why]
- How does a compass know where north is? — Earth is a giant magnet. [Way Things Work]
- Can a speaker work as a microphone? — Same coil and magnet, run in reverse. [Way Things Work]
- How does a water gun shoot so far? — Pumped air squeezes the water out. [Whoosh!]

## Chemistry
- Why is rust heavier than the iron it came from? — Iron grabs air as it rusts. [Tell Me Why]
- Why can you blow a bubble with gum but not with a sweet? — Stretchy chains hold the film. [Pop!]
- Where does a puddle go? — Water slips into the air, unseen. [Best Ever Why]

## Earth
- How does a bone turn into stone? — Minerals slowly replace it, bit by bit. [But Why]
- How does dripping water build stone icicles in a cave? — Each drop leaves one speck behind. [How Everything Works]
- Where does the rain that lands on you end up? — Follow a drop down a river basin. [How Everything Works]
- How deep is the deepest part of the ocean next to the tallest mountain? — Stack Everest in the trench. [TFK Big Book of Why]

## History
- How can a stone arch stand with no glue? — The top stone locks the rest in place. [Cathedral]

## Music
- Why does music make you want to move? — Your brain locks onto the beat. [But Why]

## Food
- How does a bitter bean become chocolate? — Ferment, roast, grind, melt. [Tell Me Why]

## Energy
- Why do wind turbines have three blades? — Fewer blades spin freely, more block the air. [How Everything Works]
- How can an electric car recharge itself when it brakes? — The motor runs backwards as a generator. [How Everything Works]
- How does a light bulb make light? — Hot wire glows, then the newer kind skips heat. [But Why]

## Cities
- What is under a city street? — Pipes, wires and tunnels in layers. [Street Beneath My Feet]

## Art
- How does a camera catch a picture? — Light through a small hole paints the scene. [Way Things Work]
