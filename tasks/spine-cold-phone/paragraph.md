**Title:** Why can your phone switch off in the cold with battery left?
**Setup:** Your phone says 30%, then switches off.

**Paragraph:**
[1] Your phone says 30%, then switches off in the freezing cold. But the battery isn't empty. The power is still inside it, just hard to get out. [2] A battery carries power as tiny charged particles, called ions, swimming through a liquid. Cold thickens the liquid, like honey, so the ions crawl and the battery can't push power out quickly. [3] Your phone can't see the charge left. It works it out partly from how hard the battery pushes. A cold battery pushes weakly, so the phone thinks it's nearly empty, and when the push gets too weak to run on, it switches off. [4] Warm it up and the ions speed up again, and the charge is back. A typical battery gives only about half its charge at freezer cold. The battery wasn't empty. It was just too cold to hand over the power.
