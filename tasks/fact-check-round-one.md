# Fact check, round one (2026-10-26)

Ten independent checkers, one per topic, on the draft copy in `Copy Review Round One.html` (birthday slide 1 reframed as the coin-flip question). Nothing was WRONG outright except where marked. Fixes below are NOT yet applied to any copy; apply them after the owner's edits are in.

## Birthday
- All maths confirmed (23 = 50.7%, 57 = 99.01%, 253 pairs, 1,596 pairs).
- "Most people guess 180" has no source: say "many people".
- World Cup 2014 (16 of 32 squads) rests on secondary sources: say "about half".
- Slide 4 ("253 tickets") is the weak step. Use: "Each ticket almost always loses. But with 253 tickets, the chances add up to about 50-50."
- Slide 4 picture: tally settles near half only over many rooms.
- Add: you need about 253 people for a 50% chance to match YOUR birthday.

## Seasons
- Slide 4 "sunlight comes straight down" misleads (London's June noon Sun is about 62 degrees up). Use: "sunlight from higher in the sky, hitting the ground more directly".
- Deeper note: North Pole "six months up, six down" is loose (refraction, twilight). Use: "up for about six months, then months of twilight and dark".
- Numbers (23.4 degrees, 3%, January/July, London day lengths) confirmed.

## Trains
- "Cone steers a gentle bend alone" is too strong: only very gentle bends (about 900 m radius or more). Use "a very gentle bend needs only a tiny shift".
- Slide 5: axle settles at a small offset toward the outside; it is not "kept pushed".
- Slide 6: "about a centimetre" becomes "a few millimetres each side".
- Squeal also comes from tread slipping, not only the flange.
- Slide 4: "keeps nudging itself back to the middle" (real trains weave).
- Say points (switches) choose the track; the cone only follows the bend.

## Gecko
- "Two 1 kg sugar bags" has no source (20 N / 43 g unverified; about 48x body weight, above measured 20-40x). Use "dozens of times its own weight" or drop.
- "A big gecko weighs as much as an egg" is wrong (tokays 150-400 g; egg about 60 g).
- Slide 3 "a bit like gravity" misleads: use "molecules pull on each other a tiny bit when they almost touch".
- Slide 4 "touch almost everywhere": use "at far more spots than a book would".
- Slide 1 "glass ceiling": use "even upside down".
- Slide 2 "millions of hairs on its toes": use "across its feet".

## Voice
- Slide 5 "sounds less deep than you expect" is contested (a UBC study found most people judge recordings as lower). Use "sounds different from what you expect"; never say higher or lower.
- Slide 4 "bone holds back high sounds" overstated; picture must not shrink the bone bars to nothing. Use "bone carries deep sounds well, high sounds less well".
- Plugged ears: "the deep part of your own voice gets louder".
- Deeper: "most probably did not realise"; the "surprise" idea was not tested: say "nobody knows for sure".
- Medium confidence: checker read abstracts, not the papers.

## Chatbot
- "Always taking the likeliest piece usually makes dull text" overstated: "can make dull text that repeats itself".
- "Many thousands of years to read it": true for modern models but model-dependent; safer "more than one person could read in a lifetime".
- Everything else confirmed. If slide 3 needs trimming, cut the "long word split" sentence first.

## Cloud
- "100 elephants" is a rough NCAR estimate for a cloud about 1 km wide: say "about" and give the size.
- Slide 3 "air slows small things the most" is the weak spot. Use "air resists falling; small things have little weight, so they sink slowly".
- Slide 4: updraft "a metre or more each second", "at least 100 times faster".
- Slide 5: "rain falls when drops grow too big for the air to carry".
- Deeper: "air around it weighs nearly the same, so it floats".

## Tickle
- Nothing wrong. Slide 4: "almost as tickly as when the robot moved it alone". Draw the 0.2 s lag to scale (0.5 s sweep cycle).
- Bold line: "A tickle is a touch your brain wasn't ready for."
- Cerebellum scan was small (about 6 people): keep "scientists think".

## Fire
- Plasma note: "large share" too strict. Use "most scientists say an ordinary candle flame is not a plasma".
- Slide 3: five ideas, likeliest to lose a reader: split or trim; "the flame is where this happens".
- Slide 5: liquid wax "stays below the flame"; "Fire isn't a material".
- "Steam" becomes "water vapour"; kettle becomes "pot of boiling water".
- Spoon-in-flame trick: add "ask a grown-up".
- Light check (two searches, no pages fetched).

## Painkiller
- Slide 2: tablet "breaks up in your gut" (most absorption is in the small intestine).
- Slide 3: healthy places build "much less" (not "very little").
- Paracetamol "barely jams the machine in most of your body".
- "More than 60 years": drop the number, keep "still being worked out".
- Aspirin: "got its name and went on sale in 1899".
- Add: paracetamol has its own limits, follow the dose on the box.
- Say "a painkiller tablet"; keep the bridge right after the school-alarm analogy.
