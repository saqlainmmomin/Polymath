## Fact recheck (2026-10-27)
Three flags, all small. Everything else matches facts.md, including all the lines on the watch list not named below.

1. **Slide 2 headline and spine 3: "It keeps only the music's loudest moments"**
   - "Music's" is wrong: it keeps peaks from the whole sound, chatter included, and can't tell the two apart. The stray chatter peaks just don't line up later.
   - "Moments" suggests the loud parts of the song in time, like the chorus. A peak is one note at one moment.
   - Go back to claim 4: "It keeps only the peaks and throws everything else away, because the loudest points are the ones most likely to survive chatter and clatter."
   - Also claim 12: "Noise adds extra peaks and hides some real ones, but stray peaks don't line up, so the right song still wins even when most codes are lost."
2. **Slide 2 text, slide 2 picture, and the bold line: "the brightest dots"**
   - A peak is brighter than the dots right around it, not one of the brightest on the whole chart. Wang picks peaks patch by patch so they cover the whole chart, so a quiet passage still keeps its peaks.
   - Small, but the picture ("only the brightest dots stay") should not use one cut-off for the whole chart.
   - Go back to claim 3: "A peak is a spot in that picture that is louder than everything right around it."
3. **Slide 5 headline and spine 9: "Only the right song has your codes in the same order"**
   - This overstates it. Noise loses most of your codes, and a wrong song can have a few line up by chance. The right song is the one where *many* line up.
   - Go back to claim 11: "In the right song, many of your codes show up in the same order and with the same spacing as in your clip; in wrong songs, matches are scattered."
