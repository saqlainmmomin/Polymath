# Fact sheet: Why doesn't instant glue stick inside its own tube?

## 1. Is the title literally true?
Mostly. A sealed tube stays liquid for months. Once opened, damp air creeps in, so the tip clogs and old tubes slowly harden.

## 2. The answer in one sentence
Superglue sets when it touches water, and the sealed tube keeps water out.

## 3. Safe claims
1. Superglue does not dry; the liquid itself links up into a solid.
   *high — https://dm.henkel-dam.com/is/content/henkel/Medical-Best-Practices-Flyer*
2. The trigger is water, even the tiny amount floating in air as vapour.
   *high — https://www.compoundchem.com/2015/10/15/superglue/*
3. Almost every surface carries a thin, invisible film of water from the air.
   *high — https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules23020465*
4. That water starts a chain: each glue molecule grabs the next, making long strands.
   *high — https://www.loctite-consumer.co.uk/faq.html*
5. A sealed tube lets no water in, so the chain never starts.
   *high — https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules23020465*
6. Makers also add a tiny dash of acid, which stops any chain that starts by accident.
   *high — https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules23020465*
7. Too much acid would make the glue slow, so the dash is very small.
   *high — https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules23020465*
8. Squeezed thin between two surfaces, every bit of glue sits close to a water film.
   *high — https://dm.henkel-dam.com/is/content/henkel/Medical-Best-Practices-Flyer*
9. A thin layer grabs in about ten seconds but takes a full day to reach full strength.
   *high — https://www.permabond.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/262_TDS.pdf*
10. A thick blob sets slowly, because water must work its way in from the outside.
    *high — https://www.permabond.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/262_TDS.pdf*
11. Skin is always a little damp, so glue on fingers sets in seconds.
    *high — https://www.compoundchem.com/2015/10/15/superglue/*
12. Leave the cap off and damp air reaches the glue, so the tip hardens and blocks.
    *high — https://www.loctite-consumer.co.uk/faq.html*

## 4. Myths to avoid
- **"It dries."** Nothing evaporates away; the glue contains no solvent and changes into a solid by reacting.
- **"Air makes it set."** It is the water in the air, not the air itself. Glue sets slower in very dry rooms.
- **"More glue makes a stronger bond."** A thick layer sets slower and ends up weaker than a thin one.
- **"Doctors close cuts with ordinary superglue."** Medical skin glues are a related but gentler recipe, made to irritate skin less.

## 5. One good number
Loctite says the gap between two parts should be at most 0.2 mm for the glue to set properly. That is about two sheets of printer paper.
*Source: https://dm.henkel-dam.com/is/content/henkel/Medical-Best-Practices-Flyer (0.008 in maximum)*

## 6. Everyday comparisons
- **A match and its striker strip.** Glue is the match, water is the strip, and the tube has no strip. Right intuition: nothing happens until the two meet.
- **A row of dominoes.** One water molecule tips the first glue molecule, and the fall runs on and on. Right for the chain reaction; misleads if it suggests one push sets a whole blob.
- **Ice forming on a pond.** A thick blob skins over first while the middle stays liquid. Right for why blobs are slow; misleads if readers think cold is the cause.

## 7. Go deeper thread
Superglue was found by accident during World War Two while chemists tried to make clear plastic gun sights. They threw it out for being too sticky. Later, in the Vietnam War, it was used to seal soldiers' wounds until they reached a hospital.
*Discovery: high. Vietnam use: medium, widely reported. Source: https://www.popsci.com/technology/super-glue-invention-mistake/*
