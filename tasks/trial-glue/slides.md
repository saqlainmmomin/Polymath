### Slide 1

### Slide 2
**Headline:** Superglue goes hard when it touches water
**Text:** White school glue goes hard as its water dries away. Superglue works the other way round. Even the moisture in the air is enough to make it go hard.
**Hook:** So where does it find water on your fingers?
**Picture:** Two drops side by side. On the left, school glue: blue water dots rise out of it and it goes hard. On the right, superglue: blue moisture dots drift in from the air and it goes hard. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** SCHOOL GLUE: WATER LEAVES · SUPERGLUE: WATER ARRIVES · MOISTURE IN THE AIR

### Slide 3
**Headline:** Your skin carries a thin film of water
**Text:** It's too thin to feel, and almost everything around you has one too. When superglue lands on it, the glue touches water straight away. That's why it goes hard on your fingers in seconds.
**Hook:** So why doesn't it go hard inside the tube?
**Picture:** A close-up of a fingertip's surface with a thin blue film on top. A glue drop lands on it and goes hard from the bottom up, where it meets the film. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** SKIN · FILM OF WATER (DRAWN MUCH THICKER) · GLUE GOES HARD

### Slide 4
**Headline:** A sealed tube lets no water in
**Text:** So the glue inside just waits, runny, for months. Leave the cap off, though, and moisture from the air creeps in. The glue at the tip goes hard and blocks it.
**Bold:** School glue goes hard when water leaves. Superglue goes hard when water arrives.
**Picture:** A tube with its cap on, glue runny inside. The cap lifts off, blue moisture dots drift into the open tip, and the glue there turns hard. The topic's one hands-on control: tap to take the cap off.
**Picture labels:** CAP ON: NO WATER IN · CAP OFF: MOISTURE CREEPS IN · HARD GLUE BLOCKS THE TIP

### Go deeper
What does the water actually do? It makes the glue's tiny building blocks hook onto each other in long chains, and long chains can't flow. Makers also add a tiny dash of acid that stops any chain that starts by accident.
