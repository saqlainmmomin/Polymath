# Trial: instant glue, stage 2 (paragraph for owner approval)

**Title:** Why doesn't instant glue stick inside its own tube?
**Setup:** It goes hard on your fingers in seconds.

**Paragraph:**
[1] But it can sit in its tube for months and stay runny. So what is it waiting for? [2] White school glue goes hard as its water dries away. Superglue works the other way round. It goes hard when it touches water. Even the tiniest bit of moisture in the air is enough. [3] And almost everything around you, your skin included, carries a film of water too thin to feel. That's why superglue goes hard on your fingers so fast. [4] A sealed tube lets no water in, so the glue just waits. Leave the cap off, though, and moisture from the air creeps in, so the glue at the tip goes hard and blocks it.

**Bold line:** Ordinary glue goes hard when water leaves. Superglue goes hard when water arrives.

**Go deeper:** What does the water actually do? It makes the glue's tiny building blocks hook onto each other in long chains, and long chains can't flow. Makers also add a tiny dash of acid that stops any chain that starts by accident.

A strict paraphrase check then showed the chain slide raised more questions than it answered ("pieces of what?", "why can't chains flow?") and that "superglue doesn't dry" clashed with "waiting for water". The chain slide moved to Go deeper. A third check stalled on "water in the air" (owner: say "moisture in the air", a phrase everyone knows) and "clogs with what?"
