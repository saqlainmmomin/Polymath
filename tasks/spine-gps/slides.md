## Slides

### Slide 1
**Headline:** How does your phone find you with no mobile signal?
**Setup:** Zero bars, and your phone still finds you.
**Text:** Out in the open, with no mobile signal at all, your phone still finds you. It doesn't need to call anyone. It only listens.
**Hook:** So what radio signal is it listening to?
**Picture:** A phone on an open hillside showing zero bars. Small satellites high above, with pulses travelling down to the phone and none going back up. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** NO MOBILE SIGNAL · YOUR PHONE · LISTENING

### Slide 2
**Headline:** Satellites keep sending a radio signal that says where they are and when it was sent
**Text:** Satellites circle Earth far above you, and each one keeps sending out a radio signal. The signal says where the satellite is and what time it was sent. Your phone catches it and sends nothing back.
**Hook:** So how does the time a radio signal was sent tell your phone a distance?
**Picture:** One satellite circling above Earth. A pulse is sent from it carrying two tags, where the satellite is and the time sent, and lands on the phone. Nothing goes back up. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** SATELLITE · RADIO SIGNAL · WHERE IT IS · TIME SENT · YOUR PHONE

### Slide 3
**Headline:** The gap between when a radio signal was sent and when it arrived is a distance
**Text:** Count the seconds from lightning to thunder, and the wait tells you how far away the storm is. A satellite's radio signal is far faster than sound, but its delay is a distance too. From straight overhead it arrives in about 0.07 seconds, shorter than a blink. Your phone compares when it was sent with when it arrived. That gap, times the speed of light, is the distance.
**Hook:** So how many satellites does it take to pin down your spot?
**Picture:** First a lightning flash, then thunder a few seconds later, with the wait marked. Then a satellite straight overhead sends a pulse that lands on the phone a flicker later, with the gap marked as the distance. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** FLASH · THUNDER · THE WAIT · SENT · ARRIVED · ABOUT 0.07 SECONDS · DISTANCE

### Slide 4
**Headline:** With three satellites, only two spots fit, and only one is on Earth
**Text:** One distance leaves a whole ring of places you could be, all the same distance from that satellite. With three satellites, only two spots fit all three distances, and only one of them is on Earth. The other is far out in space.
**Hook:** But how exact is your phone's own clock?
**Picture:** A map from above. One satellite with one ring around it and a dot circling the ring. Rings from two more satellites join. Three satellites leave two spots: one sits on the ground, and an arrow takes the other far off the page. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** SATELLITE · RING · TWO SPOTS · ON EARTH · FAR OUT IN SPACE

### Slide 5
**Headline:** Your phone's own clock isn't exact, so it listens to a fourth satellite
**Text:** One problem is left. Your phone times the arrival with its own clock, and that clock is nowhere near as exact as a satellite's. A clock a millionth of a second wrong puts you about 300 metres off. So the phone listens to a fourth satellite. That lets it work out how wrong its own clock is, and fix it.
**Bold:** Your phone doesn't need to call anyone. It listens to satellites and turns the delay into where you are.
**Picture:** Your position drifts about 300 metres off while the phone's clock is wrong. A fourth satellite joins in and the position comes back to where you are. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** YOUR PHONE'S CLOCK · SATELLITE'S CLOCK · ABOUT 300 METRES OFF · FOURTH SATELLITE

### Go deeper
The satellites' clocks are set to tick a little slower before launch. Up there, far from Earth's pull and moving fast, they would otherwise run about 38 millionths of a second ahead of clocks on the ground every day, and that would pile up into a position error of about 11 kilometres a day. Engineers fixed this before launch by changing how fast the clocks tick, one of the few places where Einstein's ideas about time are used by something in your pocket.
