# GPS, stage 2 (paragraph for owner approval)

**Title:** How does your phone find you with no mobile signal?
**Setup:** Zero bars, and your phone still finds you.

**Paragraph:**
[1] Out in the open, with no mobile signal at all, your phone still finds you. It never calls anyone. It only listens. So what is it listening to? [2] Satellites circle Earth far above you, and each one keeps sending out a radio signal. The signal says where the satellite is and what time it was sent. Your phone catches it and sends nothing back. [3] Count the seconds from lightning to thunder, and the wait tells you how far away the storm is. A satellite's signal works the same way, only far faster than sound. From straight overhead it arrives in about 0.07 seconds, shorter than a blink. Your phone compares when it was sent with when it arrived. That gap, times the speed of light, is the distance. [4] One distance leaves a whole ring of places you could be, all the same distance from that satellite. With three satellites, only two spots fit all three distances, and only one of them is on Earth. The other is far out in space. [5] One problem is left. Your phone times the arrival with its own clock, and that clock isn't as exact as a satellite's. A clock a millionth of a second wrong puts you 300 metres off. So the phone listens to a fourth satellite. That lets it work out how wrong its own clock is, and fix it.

**Bold line:** Your phone never calls anyone. It listens to satellites and turns the delay into where you are.

**Go deeper:** The satellites' clocks are set to tick a little slower before launch. Up there, far from Earth's pull and moving fast, they would otherwise run about 38 millionths of a second ahead of clocks on the ground every day, and that would pile up into a position error of about 11 kilometres a day. Engineers fixed this before launch by changing how fast the clocks tick, one of the few places where Einstein's ideas about time are used by something in your pocket.

**Most likely to lose a reader:** slide 4, "only two spots fit" with no picture in the paragraph; the cold read must test it.

**Cut from the old topic (facts §8):** the "GPS" acronym and the "about 30 satellites" count, the "satellite is a machine that orbits" definition, the school-friend analogy (thunder is the one analogy), "usually", "exactly" and "anywhere", "the time now", the 1/1000 s = 300 km example (now 1 millionth = 300 m).
