### Slide 1
**Headline:** Why does a car engine need an electric motor just to start?

**Hook:** So how does burning petrol turn an engine at all?

### Slide 2
**Headline:** Hot gas pushes a sliding plug that turns a shaft
**Text:** Inside, petrol and air burn in a metal tube called a cylinder. The hot gas pushes a sliding plug, the piston, along the tube. A rod links the piston to a bent shaft. Each push turns the shaft, like your leg on a bike pedal.
**Hook:** But can the piston push every time it moves?
**Picture:** A cut-open cylinder. Fire fills the top, the piston slides along, and the rod turns the bent shaft half a turn. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** CYLINDER · HOT GAS · PISTON · ROD · BENT SHAFT

### Slide 3
**Headline:** The piston makes four moves, and only one pushes
**Text:** First it pulls fuel and air in, then squeezes them tight. Then a spark lights them. Packed tight, the burning gas pushes the piston much harder. Last, it clears the used gas out of the engine.
**Hook:** So what moves the piston for the other three?
**Picture:** The same cylinder going through its four moves in a loop, with the move's name under it. Only the push move glows. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** PULLS IN · SQUEEZES · PUSH · CLEARS OUT

### Slide 4
**Headline:** The turning shaft drags the piston through the other three
**Text:** At the start nothing is turning yet, so nothing can drag it. The electric motor turns the shaft until a spark lights the first squeezed load.
**Hook:** So once it's going, what keeps the shaft turning?
**Picture:** The electric motor turns the shaft, which drags the piston in and squeezes. A spark lights the load, the push comes, and the motor lets go. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** ELECTRIC MOTOR · SHAFT DRAGS THE PISTON · FIRST SPARK

### Slide 5
**Headline:** A heavy wheel keeps the shaft turning between pushes
**Text:** Its weight carries the piston through the other three moves. And several cylinders take turns, so the pushes come close together and the engine runs smoothly.
**Bold:** An engine can only push once it has pulled in and squeezed its fuel, so something else turns it first.
**Picture:** A heavy wheel on the end of the shaft, and a row of cylinders above it. Each one pushes in turn, and the wheel keeps spinning steadily. The topic's one hands-on control: tap 1 or 4 cylinders: with 1 the shaft keeps turning but unevenly, with 4 it runs smooth.
**Picture labels:** HEAVY WHEEL · CYLINDERS TAKE TURNS

### Go deeper
The first engine to squeeze its fuel and air before lighting it was built by Nicolaus Otto in Germany in 1876, and it ran far better than every engine before it. Engines that squeeze their fuel and air and work in four moves still run on what's called the Otto cycle.

## Fact recheck (2026-10-27)
Checked against facts.md. Only meaning changes are flagged.

**Flags**
1. **Slide 3 text, order of events.** "Packed tight, they burn with a much bigger push. Then a spark lights them and the hot gas pushes the piston." Read in order, this says the mix burns while it is being squeezed, before the spark. Nothing burns until the spark. Go back to claim 6, "Squeezing the fuel and air into a small space before lighting it makes the burning gas press much harder, so the push is bigger.", and claim 10, "In a petrol engine, a spark plug makes a tiny spark at just the right moment to light the squeezed mix."
2. **Slide 5 text.** "And several cylinders take turns to push, so the engine carries itself." This says the cylinders taking turns are what keep the engine going. In fact the heavy wheel does that, and engines with one cylinder run too. Several cylinders make it smooth. Go back to claim 8, "That is why cars have several cylinders that take turns, so the pushes come close together and the engine runs smoothly.", and claim 5, "Only one of those four moves pushes, and a heavy spinning wheel on the shaft, called the flywheel, carries the piston through the other three."
3. **Slide 5 picture.** "tap 1 or 4 cylinders to feel the shaft lurch or run smooth". This is true only if the 1-cylinder setting keeps turning, unevenly but without stopping. A shaft that stalls or stops between pushes would contradict claim 5 above. Claim 8 above is the line for what 1 versus 4 changes: smoothness only.
4. **Slide 5 text, overstated.** "Once it spins, it's hard to stop." Without pushes an engine slows and stops within moments. The heavy wheel only carries the shaft from one push to the next. Go back to claim 5 above. The headline "A heavy wheel keeps the shaft turning between pushes" is fine.
5. **Go deeper.** "Engines that work in these four moves are still called Otto engines." The source names the cycle, not the engine. Diesels also work in four moves but are not Otto-cycle engines. Go back to "Engines that squeeze, burn, push and clear out in four moves are still called "Otto cycle" engines today." Note that this facts.md sentence is itself loose about diesels. The sourced line is "The term 'Otto cycle' is applied to all compressed charge, four cycle engines" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Otto).

**Checked and fine (no change)**
- Slide 1: "Press start, and a small electric motor turns the engine for a moment before it runs by itself." True (claim 13). "Once it's running, it keeps itself going for hours." True while it has fuel. The picture order (motor spins, engine catches, motor stops, shaft keeps turning) is right.
- Slide 2 headline, "Hot gas pushes a sliding plug that turns a shaft". The piston turns the shaft through the rod, and the slide text names the rod, so this is fine. The picture shows one push turning the shaft half a turn, which is right.
- Slide 3 picture: four moves with only the push glowing. Right (claims 4 and 5).
- Slide 4: "nothing can drag it", and the picture of the motor turning the shaft, the shaft dragging the piston in and squeezing, the spark, the push, then the motor letting go. True (claims 5 and 13). Real starters turn the engine for a few pushes rather than one, but "loops by itself" covers that.
- Bold: "An engine can only push once it has pulled in and squeezed its fuel, so something else turns it first." True for petrol, which is this topic (slides 2 and 3 say petrol). A diesel squeezes only air and its fuel is sprayed in after (claim 11). That is true enough here, because nothing in the slides mentions diesel.
- Go deeper: "built by Nicolaus Otto in Germany in 1876, and it ran far better than every engine before it." Matches facts.md section 7.
