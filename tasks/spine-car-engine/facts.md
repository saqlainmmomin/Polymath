# Fact sheet: How does a car's petrol engine turn burning fuel into turning wheels?

## 1. Is the title literally true?
- **"How does a car engine actually work?"** True, but it is the textbook framing the owner passes over. No twist. Don't use.
- **"How do tiny explosions make your car's wheels turn?"** Not safe. In a healthy petrol engine the mix burns as a fast flame that sweeps across the cylinder; a real explosion inside the cylinder is called knock, and it is a fault that can wreck the engine. Don't use "explosion".
- **"How does burning petrol push a car down the road?"** True and plain, but still a "how X works" question with no hidden reason. Safe fallback only.
- **"Why does a car engine squeeze its fuel before burning it?"** True for petrol engines, which squeeze fuel and air together; a diesel squeezes only air and sprays the fuel in after. The reader holds no belief about squeezing, so the title answers a question nobody has asked yet. Weak.
- **"Why does a car engine need to keep spinning even when you stop?"** Not literally true. Many new cars have start-stop systems that switch the engine off at traffic lights, and electric cars have no engine to keep spinning. Don't use.
- **"How can a fire inside a metal box turn a car's wheels?"** Mostly true: "fire" is fair for the burning mix, but a cylinder is a tube, not a box, and the picture of a box with a fire in it misleads. Medium.
- **Suggested twist:** "Why does a car engine need an electric motor just to start?" Literally true for petrol cars today: an engine cannot push until it has pulled in and squeezed a first load of fuel and air, so a small electric motor, the starter, has to turn it first. It points at something every driver does, turning the key or pressing start, and the answer is the whole topic: only one move in four pushes. **Best.** Alternative: "Why does a car engine hum if it runs on bursts of fire?" True: at highway speed a four-cylinder engine gets about 80 pushes a second, far too fast to hear one by one. Medium, because the link from push rate to the sound you hear is not sourced here.

## 2. The answer in one sentence
Petrol and air are squeezed and burned inside a cylinder, the hot gas shoves a piston down, and a rod and a bent shaft turn that push into spin, with several cylinders taking turns so the spin never stops.

## 3. Safe claims
1. A car's petrol engine burns a mix of petrol and air inside a closed metal tube called a cylinder.
   *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine*
2. The burning gas gets very hot and presses hard on a sliding plug called a piston, shoving it down the tube.
   *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine*
3. A rod joins the piston to a bent shaft called the crankshaft, so each push down turns the shaft, the way your leg pushing a bike pedal turns the pedals round.
   *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crankshaft*
4. Each cylinder repeats four moves: pull fuel and air in, squeeze it, burn it to push the piston down, and push the used gas out.
   *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine*
5. Only one of those four moves pushes, and a heavy spinning wheel on the shaft, called the flywheel, carries the piston through the other three.
   *high — https://courses.washington.edu/engr100/Section_Wei/engine/UofWindsorManual/Flywheel.htm (flywheel keeps the crankshaft turning "between combustion intervals"); Princeton course page on the four strokes, https://commons.princeton.edu/63-tiger-cub/wp-json/wp/v2/pages/76 (found by search; the page would not load directly, so medium on this link alone) ("only one is powered directly by the combustion")*
6. Squeezing the fuel and air into a small space before lighting it makes the burning gas press much harder, so the push is bigger.
   *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine (efficiency rises by "first compressing the fuel mixture prior to its ignition"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_ratio (petrol cars squeeze the mix to between an eighth and a twelfth of its starting space)*
7. One cylinder pushes only once every two turns of the shaft.
   *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine (the power stroke starts the second turn of the cycle)*
8. That is why cars have several cylinders that take turns, so the pushes come close together and the engine runs smoothly.
   *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight-four_engine (fewer cylinders means gaps in the pushing and more shaking)*
9. At a steady highway speed, each cylinder of a typical car engine gets about 20 pushes every second.
   *medium — arithmetic: a car at highway speed commonly turns about 2,000 to 3,000 times a minute, and one push per two turns gives 17 to 25 a second. The turns-a-minute range comes from owner reports, not a textbook; say "about 20", never an exact figure.*
10. In a petrol engine, a spark plug makes a tiny spark at just the right moment to light the squeezed mix.
    *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spark_plug*
11. A diesel engine has no spark plug: it squeezes air so hard that the air gets hot enough to light the fuel by itself.
    *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_ratio (diesels squeeze to between a fourteenth and a twenty-third)*
12. The engine's spin reaches the wheels through a set of gears.
    *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_(mechanical_device)*

Spare, if the suggested title is used:
13. An engine cannot start turning on its own, so a small electric motor spins it until the first pushes arrive.
    *high — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starter_(engine) ("the first two strokes must be powered in some other way than from the engine itself")*

## 4. Myths to avoid
- **"Tiny explosions" / "bang".** Normal burning is a flame that sweeps across the cylinder in an orderly way. A true explosion of the mix is knock, a fault that can destroy parts (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine_knocking). Say "burns" or "a burst of fire", not "explodes". "Bang" as a stroke name invites the same myth.
- **"The fuel itself pushes the piston."** The push comes from hot gas pressing on the piston. Most of that gas is air, heated by the burning; the petrol is a small part of the mix.
- **"Squeezing stores energy like a spring that then springs back."** Squeezing costs the engine work. The bigger push comes from burning the mix while it is packed tight.
- **"The piston lifts itself back up."** Nothing pushes it up; the spinning shaft and flywheel drag it through the other three moves.
- **"More cylinders always means more power."** More cylinders mainly means pushes come closer together and the engine runs smoother. Power depends on size and design, and a small four can beat an old eight.
- **"Every engine has spark plugs."** Diesels don't.
- **"The engine has to keep running when you stop."** Start-stop systems switch it off at lights; electric cars have no engine.
- **Efficiency numbers** such as "only 20% of the fuel moves the car" or "a third goes out the exhaust". Not sourced here; leave them out.

## 5. One good number
At highway speed, each cylinder gets about 20 pushes of burning gas every second, so a four-cylinder engine gets about 80. Everyday comparison: you could not say "push" even once in the time a cylinder pushes three times, so the separate pushes blur into one steady hum, the way a fast drumroll stops sounding like single taps.
*Source: arithmetic from one push per two turns, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine, and a cruising speed of about 2,000 to 3,000 turns a minute, the range most car rev counters show on the highway (owner reports; medium). Say "about 20", never more exact. Safer alternative if the owner wants a high-confidence number: petrol car engines squeeze the mix into about a tenth of its starting space (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_ratio, 8:1 to 12:1), like squashing a full 1.5 litre bottle of air down to a small glass.*

## 6. Everyday comparisons
- **A bike pedal and your leg.** Right picture for the rod and crankshaft: a push straight down becomes a turn. Misleads if the reader's next thought is "and my leg lifts itself back up": the piston is dragged back up by the spinning shaft. It also misleads on timing, since a rider pushes every half turn with alternating legs while one cylinder pushes once every two turns. **Use for the crank only, and say the shaft carries the piston back.**
- **Several riders on one long bike, taking turns.** Right for why several cylinders give smooth spin: one rider pushing once in a while makes the bike lurch, four taking turns keep it rolling. Misleads only if readers picture riders pushing all the time; each one pushes only a quarter of the time. **Use, and say each rider rests most of the time.**
- **Blowing a cork from a bottle.** Right for "trapped gas under pressure shoves a plug". Misleads three ways: the cork flies off and is gone, while the piston is tied to the shaft and comes back; it suggests a pop or explosion; and a fizzy bottle isn't burning anything. **Avoid, or use only for the first push.**
- **A squeezed spring.** Misleads: the obvious next thought is that the squeeze itself stores the energy that comes back as the push. The squeeze costs energy; the burning provides the push. **Don't use.**

## 7. Go deeper thread
The first engine to squeeze its fuel and air before lighting it was built by Nicolaus Otto in Germany in 1876, and it ran far better than every engine before it. A French engineer, Alphonse Beau de Rochas, had already patented the four-move idea in 1862 without building one, and that old patent was later used to knock out one of Otto's patents. Engines that squeeze, burn, push and clear out in four moves are still called "Otto cycle" engines today.
*1876 and "Otto cycle" name: high, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Otto; earlier squeezing engines being less efficient: high, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine ("By 1876 ... the first internal combustion engine that compressed the fuel mixture"); Beau de Rochas 1862 patent and Otto losing a patent: high, same Otto page. No date for the patent loss is sourced; don't give one.*
