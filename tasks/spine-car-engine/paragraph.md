# Car engine, stage 2 (paragraph for owner approval)

**Title:** Why does a car engine need an electric motor just to start?
**Setup:** Once it's running, it keeps itself going for hours.

**Paragraph:**
[1] So why can't it get going by itself? [2] Inside, petrol and air burn in a metal tube called a cylinder, and the hot gas pushes a sliding plug, the piston, along the tube. A rod links the piston to a bent shaft, so each push turns the shaft, like your leg on a bike pedal. [3] But the piston makes four moves, and only one of them pushes. First it pulls fuel and air in, then squeezes them tight, because packed tight they burn with a much bigger push. Then a spark lights them and the hot gas pushes the piston, and last it clears the used gas out of the engine. [4] For the other three moves, the turning shaft has to drag the piston along. At the start nothing is turning yet, so the electric motor turns the shaft until a spark lights the first squeezed load. [5] After that the engine carries itself. A heavy wheel on the shaft is hard to stop once it spins, so it keeps turning between pushes, and several cylinders take turns to push.

**Bold line:** An engine can only push after it has pulled in and squeezed its fuel, so something else has to turn it first.

**Go deeper:** The first engine to squeeze its fuel and air before lighting it was built by Nicolaus Otto in Germany in 1876, and it ran far better than every engine before it. Engines that squeeze their fuel and air and work in four moves still run on what's called the Otto cycle.

**Most likely to lose a reader:** slide 3, four moves in one slide.
