# Bluetooth, stage 2 (paragraph for owner approval), round 2

**Title:** Why doesn't Wi-Fi drown out your Bluetooth earbuds?
**Setup:** They share the same airwaves, and Wi-Fi can be far stronger.

**Paragraph:**
[1] Your earbuds get their music as a weak signal. Wi-Fi fills a room with stronger ones. But the music keeps playing. So where does a weak signal find room? [2] Picture Bluetooth's airwaves as a radio dial. One Wi-Fi network can cover about a quarter of it. Bluetooth cuts its dial into dozens of narrow channels and uses one at a time. Stay on one, and you could land on Wi-Fi. [3] So Bluetooth never stays on one. When your devices connect, they work out the same order of channels and hop through it together, up to 1,600 times a second, over a hundred in one blink. What if a hop lands on Wi-Fi? [4] Each hop carries one tiny piece of music. If it lands on Wi-Fi, only that piece is lost, and it's sent again on a later hop. If the resend is late, the music skips for a split second.

**Bold line:** Bluetooth can't win by being stronger, so it never stays in one place.
