# Bluetooth, stage 3 (slides cut from the approved paragraph)

## Spine
1. **Title:** Why doesn't Wi-Fi drown out your Bluetooth earbuds?
2. **Hook 1:** So where does a weak signal find room?
3. **Headline 2:** Bluetooth finds room in one narrow channel at a time
4. **Hook 2:** Stay on one, and Bluetooth could land on Wi-Fi.

## Slides

### Slide 1
**Headline:** Why doesn't Wi-Fi drown out your Bluetooth earbuds?
**Setup:** They share the same airwaves, and Wi-Fi can be far stronger.
**Text:** Your earbuds get their music as a weak signal. Wi-Fi fills a room with stronger ones. But the music keeps playing.
**Hook:** So where does a weak signal find room?
**Picture:** A Wi-Fi box on the left sends big white rings across the room. In the middle a phone sends a small pink wave to an earbud on the right, which lights up pink while the music plays. A pink question mark floats over the wave. Loops by itself.
**Picture labels:** WI-FI · EARBUD · WEAK SIGNAL

### Slide 2
**Headline:** Bluetooth finds room in one narrow channel at a time
**Text:** Picture Bluetooth's airwaves as a radio dial. One Wi-Fi network can cover about a quarter of it. Bluetooth cuts its dial into dozens of narrow channels and uses one at a time.
**Hook:** Stay on one, and Bluetooth could land on Wi-Fi.
**Picture:** A radio dial lying flat. A hatched block covers its left quarter (Wi-Fi). Dozens of narrow ticks fill the dial and one pink channel (Bluetooth) lights on the right. The pink channel slides left into the Wi-Fi block and is crossed out.
**Picture labels:** RADIO DIAL · WI-FI: ABOUT A QUARTER · DOZENS OF NARROW CHANNELS · BLUETOOTH: ONE AT A TIME · BLUETOOTH LANDS ON WI-FI

### Slide 3
**Headline:** Bluetooth never stays on one channel
**Text:** When your devices connect, they work out the same order of channels and hop through it together, up to 1,600 times a second, over a hundred in one blink.
**Hook:** What if a hop lands on Wi-Fi?
**Picture:** The same dial, Wi-Fi block on the left. Pale pink marks show the order of channels, some inside the Wi-Fi block. A phone above and an earbud below ride one pink channel as it hops along the order.
**Picture labels:** PHONE · EARBUD · UP TO 1,600 HOPS A SECOND · OVER A HUNDRED IN ONE BLINK · WI-FI

### Slide 4
**Headline:** One lost piece doesn't wreck the music
**Text:** Each hop carries one tiny piece of music. If it lands on Wi-Fi, only that piece is lost, and it's sent again on a later hop. If the resend is late, the music skips for a split second.
**Bold:** Bluetooth can't win by being stronger, so it never stays in one place.
**Picture:** The dial with its Wi-Fi block on the left. A pink channel carries a small pink piece. When it lands in the Wi-Fi block it is crossed out and the matching piece of a pink music line below disappears, then comes back when sent again. One piece never arrives and leaves a short dashed gap. The channel keeps hopping while the bold line is said.
**Picture labels:** LOST · SENT AGAIN · SKIP (under a row of five music tiles)

### Go deeper (the `more` card)
Hopping is older than Bluetooth. In 1942 the actress Hedy Lamarr patented an early version of the idea, but it wasn't used then. Bluetooth was designed separately, at Ericsson, in the 1990s.
