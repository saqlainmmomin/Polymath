Why doesn't Wi-Fi drown out your Bluetooth earbuds? Your earbuds get their music as a weak signal. Wi-Fi fills a room with stronger ones. But the music keeps playing. So where does a weak signal find room?

Picture Bluetooth's airwaves as a radio dial. One Wi-Fi network can cover about a quarter of it. Bluetooth cuts its dial into dozens of narrow channels and uses one at a time. Stay on one, and Bluetooth could land on Wi-Fi.

So Bluetooth never stays on one. When your devices connect, they work out the same order of channels and hop through it together, up to 1,600 times a second, over a hundred in one blink. What if a hop lands on Wi-Fi?

Each hop carries one tiny piece of music. If it lands on Wi-Fi, only that piece is lost, and it's sent again on a later hop. If the resend is late, the music skips for a split second. Bluetooth can't win by being stronger, so it never stays in one place.
