# Shazam, rewritten with this skill (2026-10-25, not yet applied to topics/shazam.js)

Words kept to one meaning: clip (your recording), song (a song in Shazam's library), chart, peak (a loud point), code, index.

### Slide 1
**Headline:** How does Shazam know what song is playing?
**Setup:** Any song. Out of millions. In a few seconds.
**Text:** You are in a noisy café. People talk. Cups clink. Music plays somewhere. You hold up your phone, and Shazam tells you the name of the song. It picks one song out of millions.
**Hook:** How? First, Shazam turns the sound into a picture.

### Slide 2
**Headline:** Shazam turns sound into a chart
**Text:** Shazam draws every sound as a chart. Time goes from left to right. High notes go near the top. Low notes go near the bottom. This height is called frequency. A loud note shows bright. A quiet note shows dim.
**Hook:** So what does your noisy café look like on this chart?

### Slide 3
**Headline:** Your café becomes a chart too
**Text:** When you tap Shazam, your phone records a few seconds of sound. This short recording is your clip. It has the song in it, and also the talking and the cups. Shazam turns the whole clip into one chart. The song makes bright lines. The noise makes faint dots everywhere.
**Hook:** That is a lot of dots. Shazam cannot keep them all.

### Slide 4
**Headline:** Shazam keeps only the loudest points
**Text:** Your chart has thousands of dots. If Shazam compares every dot with millions of songs, it is too slow, even for a computer. So Shazam keeps only the loudest points on the chart. These points are called peaks. The faint noise disappears. Only the music's peaks stay.
**Hook:** But one peak alone does not tell Shazam much.

### Bridge
One peak is just one note at one moment. Thousands of songs have that same note. But two peaks together are much rarer. Think of a note at 1,200 Hz, then a note at 2,400 Hz, exactly 0.3 seconds later. (Hz measures how high a note is.) Very few songs have that exact pair.
**Hook:** So Shazam uses pairs of peaks.

### Slide 5
**Headline:** Two peaks make a code
**Text:** Shazam joins each peak to a few peaks that come just after it. For each pair, it writes down three numbers. The first number is how high the first note is. The second number is how high the second note is. The third number is the time between them. These three numbers make one code.
**Hook:** Your clip gives Shazam lots of codes. Now it must find the song that has them.

### Slide 6
**Headline:** Shazam looks up each code, like a book's index
**Text:** Shazam does not check millions of songs one by one. That takes days. It uses a list like the index at the back of a book. A book's index shows every page that has a word. Shazam's list shows every song that has a code. One song comes up again and again, at the same times as your clip. That song is the answer.
**Closing line (bold):** Shazam never listens to the whole song. It only matches pairs of loud notes.
