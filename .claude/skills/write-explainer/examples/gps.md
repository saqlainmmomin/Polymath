# GPS, rewritten with this skill (owner-approved 2026-10-25, not yet applied to topics/gps.js)

Words kept to one meaning: satellite, signal (a radio message), clock, distance, ring.

### Slide 1
**Headline:** How does your phone know exactly where you are?
**Setup:** It finds you within a few metres, anywhere on Earth.
**Text:** Open a map on your phone. A blue dot shows where you stand. Your phone never sends a message to space to find you. It only listens.
**Hook:** So what is it listening to?
**Picture labels:** YOUR PHONE · LISTENING

### Slide 2
**Headline:** Satellites keep calling out the time
**Text:** About 30 GPS satellites circle Earth, high in space. A satellite is a machine that orbits our planet. Each one carries a very exact clock. Each one sends a radio signal, again and again. The signal says two things: "This is where I am" and "This is the time now."
**Hook:** How can a time tell your phone how far away something is? A storm shows you.
**Picture labels:** SATELLITE · WHERE I AM · TIME SENT

### Slide 3
**Headline:** A storm turns a delay into a distance
**Text:** Lightning and thunder happen at the same moment. You see the flash at once. The sound of the thunder travels much more slowly. So you hear it later. Count the seconds between the flash and the boom. Every 3 seconds means the storm is about 1 kilometre away.
**Hook:** GPS uses the same trick, but with a signal that travels much faster.
**Picture labels:** LIGHT: ARRIVES AT ONCE · SOUND: MUCH SLOWER · SECONDS SINCE FLASH · BOOM

### Slide 4
**Headline:** The satellite signal works like a very fast thunderclap
**Text:** A satellite signal travels at the speed of light. From straight above, it takes about 0.07 seconds to reach you. The signal says what time it left. Your phone notes what time it arrived. The gap between the two times tells your phone the distance.
**Hook:** But knowing a distance is not the same as knowing a place.
**Picture labels:** TIME SENT · TIME ARRIVED · SHOWN MUCH SLOWER

### Slide 5
**Headline:** One distance gives a ring of places
**Text:** Look at a map from above. A friend says, "I am 1 kilometre from the school." Where is your friend? They can be anywhere on a ring around the school. GPS has the same problem. One satellite gives one distance. One distance gives a ring of possible places.
**Hook:** So how does your phone pick the one right place on the ring?
**Picture labels:** A MAP FROM ABOVE · SATELLITE · SAME DISTANCE · YOU?

### Slide 6
**Headline:** More satellites give one place
**Text:** A second satellite gives a second ring. The two rings cross at two places. A third satellite gives a third ring. Usually, only one of the two crossing places is on it.
**Closing line (bold):** That place is you.
**Picture labels:** NOT HERE · THAT PLACE IS YOU · REAL PHONES USE 4 OR MORE

### Go deeper
The timing must be perfect. If the time is wrong by one thousandth of a second, your phone is 300 kilometres off. Phone clocks are not that exact. So your phone listens to a fourth satellite too. The extra satellite lets it correct its own clock.

Notes: "about 30 satellites" and "within a few metres" are new and not yet fact checked; the other numbers passed the earlier GPS fact check.
