Why does one gear click make the same hill feel easier? On a steep hill you push as hard as you can and barely move. Click the gear lever once and it feels easier. Your legs aren't stronger, and the hill isn't flatter. So what does the lever change at the back wheel?

The chain runs from the pedal ring to a stack of rings on the back wheel, and the lever slides it onto another one. Each link fits one tooth, so a smaller ring, with fewer teeth, spins the wheel more times for each pedal turn. So how far does one pedal turn carry you?

With a smaller back ring, each pedal turn carries you farther. With a bigger one, each turn takes you a shorter way. So why does a shorter way per turn make the hill feel easier?

Think of walking up a steep path. Long strides are hard heaves, short quick steps are light, and you reach the same top either way. A pedal turn is like a stride, and the hill is the same height in every gear. So a shorter way per turn lifts you only a little, the push can be light, and you just need more turns. But what about on flat road?

To go the same speed on flat road, a smaller back ring means pedalling slower, with a harder push each time. That's fine there, because legs push best in a narrow range of pedalling speeds. A gear can't make the hill any lower. It only decides how many pushes it takes to climb it.
