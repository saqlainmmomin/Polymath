Can your skin feel wet when it is dry? Your skin has tiny nerve endings, called sensors, that react to things such as cold, warmth and touch. But scientists have looked for one made just for wet, and found none. So what do your sensors feel when water touches you?

When something wet touches you, water cooler than your skin takes heat out of it fast. Your cold sensors notice the drop and send a cold signal to your brain. But is a cold signal all it takes to feel wet?

Water also hugs your skin, touching every spot at once and just as softly, and your touch sensors feel that. Your brain puts the cold signal and the soft touch together into wet. So could a dry finger feel wet?

It can, and you can try it. Pull a thin rubber glove onto one finger, check no water can get in, and lower it into cool water. The finger stays dry but feels wet. In lukewarm water it barely does, because the cold signal is almost gone. Wet is not a sense of its own. Your brain builds it from cold and touch.
