Why do you start sweating when you eat spicy food? The spicy part of a chili pepper is a compound called capsaicin. It switches on a heat sensor on your nerve endings, one that normally fires only for something truly hot, above about 43°C. So your brain hears "burning hot", even though nothing is. So what does your body do about a burn that isn't there?

Your body answers as if it were overheating. Blood vessels widen, your heart may beat faster, and your sweat glands start up to cool you down. You sweat most on your forehead, scalp, nose and upper lip. So is sweat the only thing that reaction sets off?

Spicy food can make your nose run and your eyes water too. It's the same reaction to the same "burning hot" message, just in other parts of you. So do people who love spicy food just put up with it?

If you eat spicy food a lot, you get used to it. Your heat sensors become less reactive over time, so the same chili sets off less. Chili tricks a heat sensor into saying "burning hot", and your body sweats as if it were true.
