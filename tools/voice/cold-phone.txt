Why can your phone switch off in the cold with battery left? Your phone says 30%, and then, in the freezing cold, the screen goes black anyway. The power is still inside, just hard to get out. So what slows it down inside?

A battery carries power as tiny charged particles, called ions, swimming through a liquid. Cold thickens the liquid, like honey. The ions crawl, so the battery can't push power out quickly. So how does your phone tell what's left?

Your phone can't see the charge left. It works it out partly from how hard the battery pushes. A cold battery pushes weakly, so the phone thinks it's nearly empty. When the push gets too weak to run on, it switches off. So is the charge gone?

No, it was never empty. As the battery warms, the ions speed up again. A typical battery gives only about half its charge at freezer cold, and all of it back at room temperature. It was just too cold to hand over the power.
